## What to write

Rewrite the brand lessons in full, with the new feedback folded in, list any NEW playbook rules this feedback suggests, and write a short reply to the person:
- Turn each point of feedback into a rule Ore can follow while writing: specific, testable, and phrased as what to do ("Every list item gets its own 'why it matters' line..."), not as a summary of what was said. Include the reason in a few words when it helps.
- Keep what the feedback praised as a rule too ("Keep: ..."), so good choices don't get lost.
- If the feedback leaves a choice open (e.g. "direct creators to vary the hook, or let them?"), pick the better option and write that as the rule. The person can edit or remove any rule, so don't mark anything as undecided.
- Merge with the existing brand lessons: don't repeat a rule that's already there; sharpen it instead. If the new feedback contradicts an old lesson, the new feedback wins; drop or rewrite the old one.
- Suggested playbook rules are only the new ones: skip anything already in the playbook or already waiting for review. It's fine to suggest none.
- Facts about the product that came with the feedback (e.g. why a feature matters) belong in brand lessons, worded as what copy should say, and only as the person stated them. Don't add facts they didn't give. Don't add rules, limits or "(compliance check)" notes the person didn't ask for: the only limits are the ones in the feedback and the brand's own rules.
- After each rule, cite where it came from in brackets, e.g. [{{source_tag}}].
- Keep the brand lessons tight: the most important rules first, at most about 25.
- The reply goes back to the person who gave the feedback. One to three short sentences, first person, plain words: say what you'll do differently from now on (e.g. "Got it. From now on every item in a list gets its own 'why it matters' line, and I've retired the sticky-notes line."). No thanks-for-the-feedback filler, no mention of brand lessons, playbooks or rules.
