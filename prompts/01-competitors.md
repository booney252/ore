# Step 1: Competitor teardown

You are researching the competitive landscape for {{product}}, a {{niche}} product sold to {{audience}} (ages {{age_range}}) in {{country}}.

## What you're working with

Use the data below first. Only go to the web for facts the data doesn't cover (pricing, revenue, user counts), and give a source link for every one of those facts.

- Competitor list: {{competitors_json}}
- Their ads, with transcripts and breakdowns: {{ads_json}}
- Customer quotes: {{voc_json}}

## For every competitor, answer:

1. **Who they're talking to.** Which buyer are they really aiming at? Age, situation, mindset. Judge it from their ads and site, not from what they claim.
2. **How they get new customers.** Paid ads, creators and influencers, organic content, search, free tools or templates, referrals, the product spreading itself. Name the main one or two.
3. **What they keep saying.** The core message across their ads, landing pages, and other marketing, in one or two sentences.
4. **Proof.** Link the specific ads or pages that show this.
5. **Repeat plays.** Hooks, angles, or big ideas that show up again and again. If they keep running something, it's probably working for them.
6. **Pricing.** Plans, prices, trials, discounts.
7. **What customers love and hate.** Pull from the customer quotes provided (reviews, Reddit, app store, comments). Use their exact words with the source link.
8. **Size.** Rough revenue and user count, if you can find a credible source. If not, say "not found." Don't guess.

## Then, across all competitors:

- The angles everyone is using (crowded)
- The angles almost nobody is using (open)
- Complaints customers keep making that no competitor's marketing answers

## Rules

- Every customer quote must come from the data provided, with its source link. Never write a quote yourself.
- If you don't know something, say so.
- If a competitor makes health or results claims that look risky for ad platforms, flag them. Don't present them as something to copy.
