PeptidePal's core buyer is a 38 to 64 year old American who already injects a GLP-1 or peptide, runs it on a patchwork of alarms, notes and spreadsheets, and has nobody they trust to organize it. Every asset sells control and clarity over that routine. None of them sell peptides or results.

## Read this first: product and guardrails

**What PeptidePal is:** a US app that tracks a person's peptide and GLP-1 doses, schedule and full protocol, and also logs food, calories and protein, in one place.

**The one-line positioning:** your whole routine, wherever it comes from, with the math done right and your food logged, on one calm screen.

**Rules every asset follows (LLMs: treat these as hard constraints):**

1. Sell tracking, clarity and control. Never sell results (weight lost, healing, younger skin, pain relief).
2. Paid Meta ads: no peptide or drug names, no injection sites, no vials, no needles, no body-part focus, no before and after. Meta also reviews the landing page and App Store screenshots, so those need neutral demo accounts.
3. TikTok paid: assume the strict reading. No "GLP", no weight, no appetite language.
4. Never give or imply dosing advice. The app records the user's own plan and does unit math on what they enter. It does not tell anyone what to take.
5. The villain is the chaos (sticky notes, spreadsheets, four Reddit tabs, a chatbot that forgets), not the doctor. 9 in 10 peptide-aware Americans say a physician's involvement raises their trust.
6. Customer quotes in this brief are for understanding the reader. Most are results claims, so never run them as testimonials.
7. Pick one segment per piece of copy. The default lead segment is the Midlife Woman adding a second compound to a GLP-1.

## 1. Demographic overview

The target is the roughly 57% of the market that sits at Problem Aware or early Solution Aware: Americans aged about 38 to 64 who already inject something, feel the friction, and patch it with alarms, notes or a calorie app plus a reminder.

**Problem Aware (about 38%, the largest stage).** They have forgotten which week they are on, deal with nausea, or worry about muscle loss. They use phone alarms, the Notes app or a notebook, and do not know an app would help. Lots of GLP-1 users here, both pen and compounded.

**Early Solution Aware (about 19%).** They know apps help. Typical setup is MyFitnessPal for food plus a phone reminder for the shot. Many compounded GLP-1 users sit here.

**Not the focus:** Unaware brand-name pen users on autopilot ("the pen does it, my doctor tracks it"), heavy in ages 55 to 65+, and Product Aware stack users who already know Shotsy or Pep AI, skewing male and 30 to 45.

**The single biggest divider is pen vs. vial.** A pen user clicks a dial once a week and the math is done for them. A vial user has to reconstitute, calculate syringe units and titrate, so the pain is unavoidable. Brand-name pens are 68% of current GLP-1 use and compounded versions are 19%. The more vials and compounds someone runs, the further along they are.

### Shared traits

- **US, cash-pay.** Has money but is cost-sensitive about healthcare, because insurance covers none of this.
- **GLP-1 use peaks in this age band.** 11% of US adults currently take a GLP-1 for weight loss (2026). By age: 11% of 30 to 49, 22% of 50 to 64, 9% of 65+. The drop at 65 is about Medicare coverage, not demand.
- **Mixed sources.** About 6 in 10 get at least some compounds through a provider, 30% buy from research websites, 14% from Telegram, WhatsApp or social sellers.
- **Self-taught.** Learns from YouTube, podcasts, Reddit and AI. 3 in 4 users in a May 2026 survey had asked ChatGPT or another AI for dosing or mixing instructions.
- **Often confused about what they are on.** Only 19% of Gen X and older say they understand what peptides are. Half of research-grade users in one survey wrongly believed their compounds were FDA-approved.
- **Often brought in by someone younger.** Older users are frequently introduced by an adult child or spouse.
- **Mostly iPhone** for the GLP-1 tracker crowd (MeAgain has about 30K iOS ratings vs 2.6K on Google Play).

**The buying moment** is going from one compound to two. A weekly pen is easy. The day they add a second vial, the math, the schedule and the fridge all break.

## 2a. Pain points

Their wins are physical and their failures are logistical: math, mixing, memory, sourcing. Nobody brags about their spreadsheet, but almost every failure story is a tracking or math failure. That is where PeptidePal lives.

**Protocol pains (what the app directly solves)**

- Lose track of what they took and when. "Wait, did I take my shot Sunday or Monday?" Terrified of double-dosing or missing a week.
- Don't trust their own math. Units, mg, mcg and IU blur together. "10 units" can mean 600 mcg or 2 mg depending on how much water went in the vial, and comment threads are full of people getting it wrong in public.
- Feel overwhelmed the moment they add a second compound. "Doing 6 injections in the morning and 1 at night." The schedule, the reconstitution dates and the fridge all stop making sense.
- Their system is a mess of sticky notes, a Google Sheet, phone alarms, four Reddit tabs and a chatbot that forgets everything. "I have to scroll so far" to find an old protocol post.
- Can't eat enough protein when appetite drops, and worry they are losing muscle along with fat. "I'm losing weight but I look soft."
- Get side effects (nausea, site reactions) and have no record to tell whether it lines up with dose timing.
- Walk into the doctor with nothing to show. "My doctors ask 'what have you been doing?'" and they can't answer cleanly.
- Waste money. A ruined first dose or a vial mixed wrong is cash they paid out of pocket. "The only harm I've experienced has been to my wallet."

**Emotional and life pains (why they are on a protocol at all; use for understanding, not as claims)**

- Scared they are doing it wrong and hurting themselves, but doing it anyway. "I'm kinda scared to feel side effects." "Their effectiveness makes me ignore, or pretend that these risks don't exist."
- Feel completely alone. Doctors are dismissive, insurance pays for none of it, and nobody gives straight answers. "Where do I find suggested dosing?" (7 likes, no replies.)
- Drowning in conflicting information. "The peptides on Reddit are so overwhelming and confusing!" "It seems no one wants to talk about specifics."
- Distrust everyone selling to them: clinics that trash the gray market then sell the same thing at 5 to 10x, fake testimonials, affiliate spam.
- Fear what is actually in the vial. "I thought I was sold something else." Independent testing found nearly 30% of research peptides mislabeled, under- or overdosed, or contaminated.
- Watched their parents decline the "normal" way and refuse that script for themselves. Feel like they are falling behind on staying young.
- Women feel brushed off. Nearly 40% of women in a 2025 survey reported being misdiagnosed when they sought care for perimenopause symptoms.
- Men feel stuck in a waiting line while their body breaks down (9 to 12 months for an MRI, "low priority" surgery).
- Losing weight but losing the look they wanted: loose skin, soft body, looking older up close than across the room.

## 2b. Hopes and dreams

They want their 35-year-old body back, and they want to feel in control of getting it. The dream has two layers: the life they want, and the calm, organized routine that gets them there.

**The life they picture (in their words)**

- **Feel younger than their age.** "I feel better at 57 than I did at 30 years old." "63 years old, my scale says I have the metabolic age of 47."
- **Avoid the knife.** Skip the hip replacement or the spinal fusion they were told was coming.
- **Look as young up close as from across the room.** "Everybody says I look 32, maybe I do from a distance."
- **Lose the weight and keep the skin and muscle.** Not "skinny", but firm and strong.
- **Get off the pill treadmill.** "I've not taken Ibuprofen or Tylenol in 2 years."
- **Be the one who figured it out.** The person their spouse, kids and friends come to for answers. Couples start together; adult kids follow a parent's lead.
- **Stay present and useful** for their spouse, kids and grandkids, and keep doing the things they love.
- **Not depend on a system that ignores them.** Self-reliance is a dream in itself.

**What a solved day looks like with PeptidePal**

They wake up and open one app. It already shows what is due today, what they took yesterday, and where they are in the week. The math on every vial is done and matches the units on their syringe, so there is no second-guessing. Lunch is logged in seconds and they can see they are on track for protein even on a low-appetite day. The fridge, the schedule and the notes app chaos are gone.

At their next appointment they hand the doctor a clean history, and for once the doctor takes them seriously. When a friend asks "how do you keep track of all this?", they pull out their phone and show them. They feel calm, competent and in charge of their own health, instead of anxious and winging it.

## 2c. How they see themselves, and how to talk to them

They see themselves as smart, self-reliant adults who were failed by the system and decided to take charge of their own health. Not patients, not biohackers, not gym bros.

**Their core beliefs**

- **Life:** Nobody is coming to save my health, so I have to become my own doctor. The people who should have helped (doctors, pharma, the FDA) have been slow, greedy, or both.
- **Love:** Taking care of my body is how I stay present for my spouse, kids and grandkids. When I find something that works, I share it.
- **Family:** I watched my parents decline the "normal" way and I refuse that script for myself.

**Attitudes that shape the copy**

- "Do your own research" is a moral value, not a habit. They respect people who show their work and distrust anyone who hides sourcing or gatekeeps.
- Proof beats credentials. Lab tests, bloodwork and daily logs earn trust. A doctor who openly uses peptides earns instant trust; a generic "white coat" does not.
- Anti-establishment more than partisan. Distrust of institutions, not party politics.
- Aging is a problem to manage, not fate to accept. "Healthspan" and "longevity" are the respectable words now.
- Cash-pay and value-conscious. Resent price gaps and clinic markups.
- Believe peptides are "natural" and safer than drugs. Strong, mostly unearned, and a compliance risk if copy leans on it.
- Split on overseas suppliers. Half fear contamination, half defend them hard. Never take a side.
- Faith is not a visible driver. Don't use faith framing.

### The true story to tell instead of a conspiracy

Before September 2023, many of these compounds came from licensed compounding pharmacies, mixed and labeled with a dose. Then the FDA restricted more than a dozen of them, demand moved to powder vials, and a 60-year-old now does the concentration math alone at the kitchen counter. Diabetics have logged every peptide injection since insulin in 1922 and got logbooks, meters, pens and apps. Today's users got a forum thread. Copy line: "Since 2023, the math moved from the pharmacy to your kitchen counter."

## 2d. Primary promises

Every promise below is about control, clarity and the routine, which is what PeptidePal can honestly and compliantly deliver. The emotional promises are the payoff of the functional ones, never a health outcome.

**Functional promises**

- Know exactly what you took, when you took it, and what's next. Never ask "did I do it Sunday or Monday?" again.
- Your whole routine in one place: every compound, every schedule, wherever it comes from.
- The math, done right. Units, mg, mcg and IU matched to your syringe, so you stop second-guessing.
- Go from sticky notes, spreadsheets, alarms and four Reddit tabs to one calm screen.
- Hit your protein every day, even on low-appetite days. Log a meal in seconds.
- Your schedule and your meals on one timeline, so you can see how your week actually went.
- Walk into your doctor's office with receipts: a clean history that gets you taken seriously.
- Reminders that keep you on schedule, so you never miss or double up.
- Simple enough for someone who "isn't a tech person." Big, clear, calm.
- Your routine, your data. Never sold.

**Emotional promises (the payoff)**

- Feel in control of your health again, instead of winging it.
- Stop being scared you're doing it wrong.
- Stop feeling alone with a routine nobody helped you set up.
- Be the one who has it figured out, the person friends and family come to.
- Peace of mind that your money and effort aren't being wasted on mistakes.
- Confidence when you add something new to your routine, not panic.
- Stay on track with the routine you chose, so you can stay strong and present for the people you love.

**Promises we can't make:** weight loss, healing, younger skin, pain relief, muscle gain, or that any compound works or is safe. The app also cannot promise to tell anyone what dose to take.

## 3. Objections and how to answer them

The two that will kill the most conversions are "my notes app works fine" (Problem Aware) and "why pay when free trackers exist" (Solution Aware). Lead with those.

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "My alarm and Notes app work fine." | I don't have a tracking problem, I have a system. | Show the breaking point, not the features. The system works until the second compound, a dose change, or a travel week. Dramatize the mess (three notes, a sheet, a sticky on the fridge) next to one screen. |
| 2 | "Why pay? There are free trackers, and MyFitnessPal logs shots for free now." | A shot log is a commodity. | Don't sell "log your shot." ## 4. What they have already tried, and why it falls short

The market already uses about a dozen tools to run a protocol, and none of them handles tracking, correct math and food in one place in a way a 55-year-old trusts. The common workaround is "cobble it together myself," which is exactly where the errors come from.

| What they use | Who uses it | What they like | Why it falls short |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brand-name pen + doctor | Unaware, mostly 55+ | Math is done; feels handled | Breaks the moment they add a second compound or go compounded. No food or protein tracking. |
| Phone alarms and calendar reminders | Problem Aware | Free, already on the phone | A reminder isn't a record. No history, no doses, no math. (73% of GLP-1 users in a UK survey struggle between appointments; over a third rely on alarms.) |

| Google Sheets / spreadsheets | Problem to Solution Aware | Total control | Breaks with 3+ compounds, math errors, no reminders. Users switch apps after tracking injections in Sheets. |
| MyFitnessPal, Lose It!, Cal AI (plus a reminder) | Early Solution Aware | Big food database; MyFitnessPal added free GLP-1 dose logging in April 2026 | Built for one GLP-1, not mixed routines. Barcode and features moved behind paywalls, ads, errors. Don't speak to non-GLP-1 users at all. |
| GLP-1 trackers (Shotsy, MeAgain, Glapp, GlucoPal) | Solution to Product Aware | Easy dose charts, Apple Health sync, all-in-one feel | Built for one or two meds. Shotsy's premium is missing promised calorie and protein charts and has data-loss complaints. MeAgain is broad but shallow, subscription-only, timestamp bugs. |
| Peptide trackers (Pep AI, Peptide Tracker, PeptIQ, Regimen, PepTracker, others) | Product Aware, skew 30 to 45 male | Reminders, reconstitution calculators, vial inventory, site rotation | "Too fiddly (and I'm a tech person)." Most have weak or no food logging. |
| ChatGPT and other AI | 3 in 4 users | Instant, patient, no judgment | No memory of their protocol, sometimes wrong, no reminders, no history. |
| YouTube doctors and creators | Everyone | Feels like a mentor | Won't give specifics ("Where do I find suggested dosing?"), often selling a clinic or course. |
| Reddit, Discord, Facebook groups | Everyone | Real experiences | "So overwhelming and confusing," contradictory, secretive, full of affiliate spam. |
| Telehealth and longevity clinics (Hims, Eden, Hone, Maximus, Ro) | Provider-sourced users | Prescription, labs, feeling listened to | Expensive ($39 then about $145 to $175/month plus meds), slow, limited catalog. **The gap PeptidePal fills:** a simple app that handles a whole mixed routine from any source, gets the units right, and pairs it with protein-first food logging. Nobody markets that to the 38 to 64 year old buyer today. The closest product is Pep AI (launched February 2026), so every asset needs to show the difference in the first 3 seconds: simpler, correct units, deeper food, reliable.

## Open questions before writing copy

The three source reports disagree in a few places, and some answers depend on product facts only the PeptidePal team has.

- [ ] **Drug names vs. compliance.** The awareness report says to lead with GLP-1 drug names for anyone over 45. The compliance rules ban drug names in Meta ads. Working answer: use "your weekly shot" in paid, save drug names for organic and SEO.
- [ ] **Is there a real free tier?** This decides how objection 2 is answered. Without one, never fight on price.
- [ ] **Data and privacy.** Where is data stored, is it ever shared or sold, can users export it? Copy can only promise what is true.
- [ ] **What the in-app AI (if any) will and won't answer.** Your protocol, your math, your history: yes. What dose to take: no. Get Vik and Michael aligned on this line before any "ask questions" messaging.
- [ ] **Which App Store listing is ours.** At least four apps use the PeptidePal or PeptiPal name, and one roundup calls a "PeptidePal" dead (3.4 stars, not updated since February 2026).
- [ ] **Regulatory dates.** The reports differ on BPC-157 (one says removed from the FDA restricted list on April 15, 2026; another says an advisory panel voted in July 2026 and the FDA hasn't finalized). Verify before any copy references a regulatory date beyond the September 2023 restriction.
- [ ] **Reddit and Facebook threads were not read directly.** Pull 20 to 30 threads from r/Peptides and the "peptides over 40" Facebook groups to confirm the language before locking copy.
- [ ] **Awareness percentages are estimates**, not survey numbers. PeptidePal's own user ages and pen vs. vial split would sharpen them.
