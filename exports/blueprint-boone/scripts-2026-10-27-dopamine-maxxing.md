# Blueprint Boone: Real Dopamine-Maxxing + 2 standalones

Built from the Oct 27 report. The format is the one that took @coachkyle (usual 4K views) to 390x and @kolbykirschner (usual 2K) to 423x, from a base the size of yours (1.7K).

## The spine every episode follows

| Beat | Time | What happens | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Title box** | whole video | Top third of the frame: a white box with the title, over a photo panel that changes on every beat. You're chest-up underneath, phone propped, bookshelf or desk behind you, one-word captions at chest height. | Kolby #10, Kyle #7, #12, #13 |
| **1. Welcome + filter** | 0-6s | "Welcome to real dopamine-maxxing, and if you [the thing everyone does], keep scrolling." | Kyle #12: "please keep scrolling… so the legends that are still here" |
| **2. Name the episode** | 6-9s | "So for the guys still here, episode [n] is [the named idea]." A named idea is saveable and shareable. | Kyle's named mini-frameworks ("the Big Boy 5", "fit in for framework") |
| **3. The mirror** | 9-25s | One moment from his life so exact he thinks "that's me". Write to "you", never about you. | Kolby #10's identity path; Austin #22 opening on the viewer's life |
| **4. Why it happens** | 25-40s | The idea in plain words, as your own way of seeing it. No book, no author, no quote. | Your rule |
| **5. The tool** | 40-60s | One thing to do, with a name and steps. Then your proof: your real week building Solis. | Kyle's "here is how", Kolby's "you've just found your people" |
| **6. Comment ask + sign-off** | last 5-8s | A question he answers in the comments (no freebie needed yet), then the same sign-off every episode. | Kyle's comment keyword took comments from 0.04% to 0.86% |

**Title-box template:** "Real Dopamine-Maxxing: [episode name] (not for [the wrong guy])". Kyle's "(not for doomscrollers)" tag does two jobs: it calls out who it's for, and it makes the right guy feel chosen.

**Sign-off (same every episode):** "That's real dopamine-maxxing. See you in the next one."

**Your proof lines are in [brackets].** Fill them with your real week. They're what makes it yours and not a philosophy page.

**Length:** 50-75 seconds. Kyle's winners ran 49-146s, so packing beats in wins over keeping it short.

## Ep 1: The Gym Rule (for guys who feel behind)

**Title box:** "Real Dopamine-Maxxing: The Gym Rule (for guys who feel behind)"
**Panels, in order:** a guy flexing at a mirror looking disappointed → a calendar full of crossed-out days → a notebook with 3 lines written → a progress photo timeline

> Welcome to real dopamine-maxxing, and if you want to lock in for nine days and crash again like every other guy on this app, keep scrolling.
>
> So for the guys still here, episode one is the gym rule.
>
> You'd never walk out of the gym, look in the mirror and go, well no six pack, guess that workout was useless. But that's exactly how you're judging your days. You put in eight hours, it hasn't paid anything yet, so you go to bed feeling behind.
>
> You're grading your day on the payoff, and the payoff always shows up late, so every single day feels like a loss even when it wasn't.
>
> So here's the gym rule. Before bed you write down three things you actually did today, and you only count the reps, whatever they paid. Today mine were [real Solis task], [the gym], and [one more].
>
> If you hit your three, that day was a win and you go to sleep like it. The six pack comes from stacking those days, and staring at the mirror never added one.
>
> Comment your three from today. That's real dopamine-maxxing. See you in the next one.

## Ep 2: The "Until" Trap (not for "someday" people)

**Title box:** "Real Dopamine-Maxxing: The 'Until' Trap (not for someday people)"
**Panels:** a finish line → a guy at a desk staring at his phone → a sticky note reading "I'll be happy when…" with a line through it → friends laughing at dinner

> Welcome to real dopamine-maxxing, and if your plan is to be happy once you finally make it, keep scrolling, 'cause this one's going to annoy you.
>
> So for the guys still here, episode two is the until trap.
>
> I'll be happy when I hit ten K a month. I'll be happy when I move out. I'll be happy when the app takes off. Every one of those sentences is you signing up to be unhappy until then, and "then" keeps moving every time you get close.
>
> I caught myself doing it with [a real Solis milestone]. I hit it, felt good for like a day, and then the sentence just changed to the next number.
>
> So here's the fix. Every time you catch yourself saying "I'll be happy when," you rewrite it out loud as "I'm happy while." I'm happy while I'm building this. I'm happy while I'm getting in shape. The goal stays exactly the same, you just stop holding your whole life hostage until you get there.
>
> Comment the "I'll be happy when" you say the most. That's real dopamine-maxxing. See you in the next one.

## Ep 3: The Should Flip (for guys who keep falling off)

**Title box:** "Real Dopamine-Maxxing: The Should Flip (for guys who keep falling off)"
**Panels:** a to-do list full of "should" → a guy lying on the couch → the same list rewritten → a camera on a tripod

> Welcome to real dopamine-maxxing, and if you're good with locking in every Monday and falling off by Thursday, keep scrolling.
>
> So for the guys still here, episode three is the should flip.
>
> Read your to-do list out loud. I should film. I should go to the gym. I should work on the business. Every line sounds like a chore somebody assigned you, and you're not going to keep doing chores for months, nobody does.
>
> So you flip the words. "I should film" turns into "I get a shot to change my whole life with one video." "I should go to the gym" turns into "I get to build the body I've wanted since I was fifteen." Same list, but now it's stuff you want.
>
> I did this with my list for Solis [real example: the task you kept avoiding and what it became], and it's the first week in a while I didn't skip it.
>
> Tonight, rewrite tomorrow's list with "I get to" in front of every line. Comment the one you flipped. That's real dopamine-maxxing. See you in the next one.

## Standalone 1: The Deathbed Test

**Title box:** "The Deathbed Test (3 questions before you say yes to anything)"
**Panels:** an old man's hands → a calendar with tomorrow torn off → a fork in a road → a checklist with 3 boxes

> If you're 18 to 25 and you keep saying yes to stuff you regret, or no to stuff you regret even more, I'm going to give you the three questions I run every opportunity through.
>
> Question one: am I going to regret not doing this when I'm old and looking back? Most of what you're scared of fails this one, which means you should do it.
>
> Question two: if tomorrow wasn't promised, would I still be doing this today? That kills most of the stuff you're doing just to look busy.
>
> Question three: is there something better I'd have to give up to say yes? 'Cause every yes is a no to something else.
>
> I ran [a real decision you made this year, like going all in on Solis or turning something down] through these three, and it was the easiest call I've made.
>
> Save this for the next time you're stuck on a decision, and comment the one you're stuck on right now.
