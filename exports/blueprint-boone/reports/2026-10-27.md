# Winning Posts Report: Blueprint Boone

25 winning organic posts from 6 accounts (@chriswillx, @austingeorgas, @greglav, @coachkyle, @kolbykirschner and Sam's own @sambooneee), all Instagram Reels, all Grade A. Today: 2026-10-27.

## 1. The big picture

### What runs through everything

**1. Reframe a feeling he already has (#2, #3, #4, #9, #15, #17, #22, #25, plus #7, #13, #18 in fitness).** Almost every big outlier takes a feeling the viewer already has and flips what it means. Fear of failing becomes "Your worst case scenario is everyone else's reality" (#4, #25). Struggle becomes the price of what you asked for: "this is exactly what you asked for" (#22). Self-pity becomes "They're just big because they're yours" (#15). Can't pick a path becomes "choose which regret you live with" (#3). Pain becomes a badge: "the best man have been broken" (#17). Wanting it badly becomes proof: "the version of you in the future already has it" (#2). This works on the Problem Aware guy because he blames himself and has no name for what's wrong. A reframe gives him relief without telling him to want less, which is exactly what the brief asks for ("the plan was broken, not you"). It's also Sam's new content direction almost word for word. #4/#25 and #15 are "your reference point decides how you feel" (series 1). #22 and #3 are "you choose your fights, so own them" (series 4). #9 is "I'll be happy when... is choosing to be unhappy" (series 2). The market has already proven Sam's first series.

**2. Say who it's for in frame 0 (#4, #7, #10, #12, #14, #19, #21).** "If you are 20-27 y/o please listen" (#14), "For all overthinkers" (#4), "Hear me out, there's a certain type of person..." (#10), "POV: Your bloodline waited centuries for a mf like you to wake up." (#19), "(not for doomscrollers)" (#7, #13), "please keep scrolling... So the legends that are still here" (#12). The viewer picks himself in before a word is spoken. For a guy who sees himself as "not like everyone else," being named is the hook. #10 is the clearest case: a 1,957-view account hit 827,782 views (423x) by describing one identity arc so exactly that people felt seen.

**3. Give the idea a name (#7, #9, #10, #12, #13, #14, #18).** "the potential resume" / "(the law of potential)" (#14), "Frankl's inverse law" (#9), "a confident B plus body" (#7, #13, #18), "the fit in for framework" (#7, #12, #18), "the Big Boy 5" (#13), "Realistic Weapon-Maxxing" (#12). A named idea feels new and is easy to repeat to a friend. The brief's mechanism ("the grind version is built to break") has no name yet. The winners suggest it needs one.

**4. Ambitious but realistic (#7, #12, #13, #18, #9).** @coachkyle's whole run of 100x to 390x outliers sells the exact position Blueprint Boone wants: "building habits that last the next five years so you never need to think about locking in again, that is even cooler" (#18), "I don't want you to overload your plate and lock in" (#7), "If you wanna lock in and train six days a week... like every other influencer on this app, please keep scrolling" (#12), and the close "be better, not perfect" on every post. He aims it at men over 30 and keeps it to fitness. #9 says the same thing to driven men from the other side: "delayed gratification in the extreme results in no gratification." Sustainable lock-in is winning big. Nobody is saying it to 18 to 25 year old builders.

**5. Proof you can see (#5, #11, #20, #19, #21, #6).** @greglav's founder posts win on real footage: "So this is 1,500 cans per minute getting filled" (#5), "We don't own this manufacturer" said inside the manufacturer (#11), a real packaging meeting with real pushback (#20). Sam's own best post (#21) is real camera roll footage under a text flip. For a viewer who "can smell a guru," seeing the thing beats being told about it.

**6. A comment keyword is the only thing that lifts comments (#8, #12, #13).** Almost every winner pulls comments at or below its account's usual rate: they spread on watch time, saves and sends. The exceptions are @coachkyle's posts with a keyword: "just comment, weapon and I'll send you all of these workouts" took #12 to 0.86% comments vs his usual 0.04% (6,198 comments). #8 ("comment 'sleep'") and #13 ("comment 'gains'") did the same. If Sam wants comments, the keyword is the lever. The reframe alone won't get them.

**The emotions:** relief (you're not broken, the bar was wrong), being seen (that's me), and aspiration (the life in the frame). Fear shows up only as one stake line (#14's 30-year-old who waited, #9's "tomorrow never arrives"), then hope. That's the brief's "fear is salt" balance.

**Against Blueprint Boone's own rules:**
- Sam's note says never name the book or the author and never open with a quote. Several winners do exactly that. #3 ("Kikigad said this 200 years ago"), #9 ("There's a famous Victor Frankl, quote" plus Alan Watts) and #24 ("Alexander the Great dropped the hardest quote of all time") name the source. #17 opens on "he has this line." #2 ("Someone said to me") and #4/#25 ("I saw a tweet that said") keep the source anonymous, but they still lead with the quote, not with the viewer's life. Use the reframe underneath these posts, not the quote-led opening.
- The brief says never shame the 9 to 5 guy. #4 and #25 ("you get a job like everyone else... Your worst case is their reality") lean on that.
- The brief says no flex without honesty. #19 is pure status (G-Wagon, boardroom, physique) with no honesty beat.
- The brief bans therapy and wellness words. #18 uses one ("a calmer nervous system").

### Emerging vs fading

These are organic posts, so "emerging" means what's in the Recent winners lane (the last 30 days) and "fading" means styles that win big elsewhere in the set but don't show up there.

- **Emerging: a talking head with a top-third image panel.** #10, #12 and #13 in the last 30 days, plus #7, #8 and #18 earlier. A plain talk to camera, with photos, tables or memes swapping in the top third of the frame for each point and a title box naming the idea. It's how two small accounts (@kolbykirschner's usual 1,957, @coachkyle's usual 4,366) beat their usual by 100x to 423x. Sam's usual is 1,685, so this is the format proven at his size.
- **Emerging: series packaging plus a comment keyword.** #12 ("This is part two"), #13 and the "-Maxxing" titles. A series earns follows, and the keyword earns comments.
- **Emerging: an idea voiceover over cinematic b-roll.** #9 (67 cuts in 109s, one word on screen at a time) turns a book-style idea into a mini film. #17 and #23 use the same edit.
- **Possibly fading: the raw single-take quote selfie.** @austingeorgas's posts are some of the biggest in the set (#2, #4, #14, #15, #22, #24, #25, all 26x to 73x his usual), but none of them are in the last-30-days lane. The data doesn't say whether the format is wearing out or he just hasn't posted one lately. The format still works. Expect it to get crowded.
- **Not for Boone either way: podcast story clips** (#1 Ted Bundy, #16 savant syndrome). They're huge, but the pull is the story and the studio, not anything Sam can repeat.

What it means for Blueprint Boone: the book-wisdom reframe is proven. The fresh packaging around it is the image-panel talking head with a named idea, a series and a keyword. Sam's first series should launch in that format, not as a bare quote selfie.

### What carries across formats

1. **The text-only flip from b-roll goes onto a talking head.** #21 (Sam's own), #6 and #19 win with one static line over footage. That same line works as the frame-0 overlay on a car selfie, like #14's "If you are 20-27 y/o please listen." The line does the stopping and the talk does the teaching.
2. **The top-third image panel goes onto a car or room selfie.** #10 and #13 put photos above a plain talking head to give sound-off viewers something to watch. It works on #14's single-take car format with zero editing skill: one photo per beat (the Solis dashboard, the gym, the boys).
3. **The comment keyword goes onto a montage.** #21 got 2 comments. #12 shows that a keyword in the caption or on screen ("comment weapon for the full workout") turns watchers into commenters. A camera roll montage with "comment MORNING for the 3 rules" would test that.
4. **The fake ending and time jump go into a personal story.** #1's "That was the whole date. They wound up getting married. And 10 years later..." resets attention halfway. It carries into any story Sam tells about his own week: "and that was it, I thought. Then Monday..."
5. **The named law in parentheses goes onto any format.** #14's "(the law of potential)" under the hook line. A one-line name under the overlay works on b-roll, a selfie or a slideshow cover.
