## 1. The big picture

### What runs through everything

**1. The product is held back until the last slide, and the slides before it make you want to know "what is it?"**
- Ads: #1, #2, #5, #6, #11, #12, #13, #14, #15, #16, #21, #22, #23, #24, #25, #26, #27. That is nearly every TikTok Shop winner.
- Slide 1 shows a result, a reaction or a problem with no product in sight. Slide 2 (or the middle slides) raises the question. The last slide answers it with the product.
- This is @brockstaaa's own formula: dream result, then the before or the secret, then the product on the vanity. The skeleton isn't what separates a 1x post from a 5,000x post. What fills the middle slides does.
- Why it works on this buyer: she's scrolling for ideas, not shopping. An opening with no product looks like a friend's post, so no ad radar fires. In awareness terms, almost every winner treats the viewer as unaware or barely problem-aware and opens indirectly (story, secret, overheard dialogue). None opens with an offer.

**2. Someone else reacts to the result.**
- Ads: #5, #6, #11, #14 (fake texts from a partner or friend), #12 ("my husband WHO NOTICES NOTHING"), #1 (the partner voices the objection), #25 (caption "the girls were NOT lying about this...").
- In the other ads list: "psa don't wear this around the boy you like if you don't want him falling in LOVE" (5.3M) and "the combo i put on every time to leave him OBSESSED." (897K).
- @brockstaaa's own winners use the same lever: the fake text "GIRLL did you get your lips done" and "girl WHAT do you put in your hair". Those questions from friends are the account's best hooks.
- Why it works: the deep want isn't "shiny hair". It's being noticed, asked about and envied. A third party saying it is more believable than the creator saying it, and it shows the status payoff directly.

**3. A price anchor, with the real price hidden or played down.**
- Ads: #1 ("we can't afford a 1200 mattress again"), #2 ("$119.98" struck through to "$35.99", "-70%"), #4 and #12 ("HUNDREDS of dollars"), #6 ("Total $98.33"), #7 ("(Sorry about the price)"), #3 and #17 ("5 for 1 deal", "price of just one"), #8 ("£275 / Worth over £1,200"), #14 and #21 ("Dirt cheap rn").
- Why it works: the brief's buyer wants "blowout hair, glowy skin, fuller lips without salon or filler money." The expensive alternative named first makes a cheap product feel like a win, not a compromise. @brockstaaa's filler hook ("the filler look without the filler money") is this exact move, and it was a 6x winner.
- Several winners never state the price at all (#4, #7, #12). The gap sends viewers to the link.

**4. An identity call-out in the hook.**
- Ads: "people with back pain" (#2), "If your a night sweater" (#13), "If you love your girl..." (#15), "A fall scent for fall boys🍂" (#16), "For the girl who loves makeup" (#10), "For the type a girl" (#19), "CROC COLORS FOR YOU" (#30).
- Why it works: the viewer either sees herself ("that's me") or sees someone to send it to ("that's so her"). Both drive shares beyond followers.

**5. The "cheap means bad" doubt gets killed in one line, then proved.**
- Ads: #4 and #12 ("The quality must not be that good right? NOPE! Literally so soft."), #2 and #13 (✅ checklists: "stays cool", "even support", "stays in place"), #23 ("didn't bother my sensitive teeth"), #26 ("It tastes like a strawb refresher too").
- Review screenshots appear in #4 and #13. Hand-in-frame texture shots appear in #1, #4 and #12.
- Why it works: a viral cheap find triggers one doubt above all others: is it junk? Winners answer it right before the close.

**6. Season-coded mood.**
- Ads: #7 ("October calls for lace undershirts 🍂🤎🍁"), #16 (four pumpkin-patch slides), #4 (🍂 and a rust palette), #8, #9, #10, #19, #20 (Christmas), #18 (fall baking), #21 and #27 (summer heat).
- Why it works: the season gives a reason to care now, and a matching palette makes the product look like it belongs in the moment.

**7. Saveable lists.**
- Ads: #3, #8, #9, #10, #17, #18, #19, #20, #28, #29, #30.
- The non-shop lists lift views 2x to 21x while comments stay flat at the account's usual rate. The lift is coming from saves, shares and search, not conversation.
- Why it works: gift-season shoppers screenshot lists. "Wishlist & gift ideas for her" (#19, #10) serves the gift-giver and the girl building her own list at once. That matters because the brief says 54% planned to treat themselves.

**8. Sets and multipacks.**
- Ads: #3, #4 ("All 7 pieces"), #7 ("5 pack"), #12 (✅ checklist of contents), #17, #8 (advent calendars).
- Why it works: a count reads as value at a glance. It also matches the brief: "Sets win."

**What the winners skip.** Almost none name a mechanism. #13 says "BAMBOO" and #26 names "berberine & yerba mate", but in #5, #11 and #14 the product label does the explaining. None offers a guarantee, and few state a price. For this buyer at this price point, the proof is a reaction, a photo or a review, not an explanation.

**Emotions:** envy and aspiration first, then curiosity, then cozy, then relief. Fear and disgust openings (#13, #21, #23, #27) do appear among the winners. @brockstaaa's own data says those flop for this account (see the rule notes on each).

### Emerging vs fading

**Emerging** (in the last-month lane or launched fresh in the other ads):
- **Black Friday deal framing:** #1 and #2 carry #blackfriday and #falldeals; nemo_brawl has "Based holiday sale". It's just starting, and no beauty Black Friday winner exists yet.
- **Christmas lists, wishlists and advent calendars:** #8, #9, #10. Also "Stocking Stuffer Ideas" (811K) and "Christmas gift ideas for tweens and teens" (428K).
- **Sets and multipacks:** #3, #4, #7.
- **The "$X per child" group-chat gift-budget story:** eddiesfinds at 6.4x, 5.5x and 4.1x its usual. These are early signals still under 10K views, but the account is the same one behind #1 and #2.
- **"psa don't give her this unless you want her bawling her eyes out":** 5.8x usual, still under 10K. Next to it, "If you love your best friend.." hit 1.98M (663x). Sentimental gift journals are heating up.
- **Fake "you're fired" pricing-mistake emails:** all Grade C so far (3,744 down to 257 views). This is new but unproven.

**Fading or wearing out:**
- **Weight-loss before/afters for supplements** (#22, #24, #25, #26): all 3-6 months old and summer-tied, with nothing similar in the last-month lane. shopturbopiggy has moved on to teeth and legs.
- **Summer car sunshade** (#21, #27): seasonal, and gone.
- **The intimacy-gummy fake text:** proven, but wearing out fast. "not to be weird but why did you feel unreal last night" did 12.9M (#11, 1-3 months ago), then 3.1M (#5, last month). Fresh copies now land at Grade C: 8,847, 3,478, 75,518 (1.3x) and 57,151 (1.0x). Same hook, falling returns.
- **The "pocket change" fake social post:** #2 hit 4M last month, but copies launched since are flopping (PAVOI advent calendar 11,025 at 1.6x; Pacsun jeans 231 views). The template alone isn't the engine. The broad pain plus a real -70% was.
- **Halloween and boo baskets:** done. The brief already says to drop Halloween.

**What this means for @brockstaaa:**
- The three-slide formula is the proven base, and competitors with usual views of 764 to 1,435 (#1, #2, #11, #12) are hitting millions with it. The account's ~950 usual is not the ceiling.
- The difference is in the middle slide: a reaction, an anchor, an objection killed.
- Hooks copied word for word are wearing out within weeks (gummies, pocket change). The structure underneath travels. The exact words don't.

### What carries across formats

1. **The fake text with a third-party reaction (#5, #11, #14).** It works as a slide 1 here. The same exchange works as on-screen text over b-roll in a video hook, or as the whole image of a static ad. The power is in who's talking, not the medium.
2. **The two-number price card, "£275 / Worth over £1,200" (#8).** It's a ready-made static headline or a video lower-third. It works for any set or advent calendar.
3. **"The quality must not be that good right? NOPE!" (#4, #12).** It's a spoken objection-handler in UGC, and a subhead on a static.
4. **Hand-in-frame texture proof** (#1 slide 4, #4 slide 3, #12 slides 2 and 7). It's a 2-second demo beat in video and a close-up panel on a static.
5. **"For the girl who..." persona chapters (#10, #19).** They become chapters in a gift-guide video or tiles in a static carousel.

**Why the images work**
- Slides 1-2 look like a real couple's date-night photos, so the dialogue reads as overheard, not scripted.

- "My back is killing me" is how people actually talk.
- "we can't afford a 1200 mattress again" adds one hard number and a backstory word.
- "TikTok Shop said "no problem"" turns the reveal into a joke with a payoff.
- The last two slides are under six words each.
- Why viewers swipe to the end: tension (pain), then a blocker (money), then "how did they solve it?". After that, each slide rewards the swipe.

**Why it's working now**
- It pairs a near-universal pain with the money conversation every couple has, then solves both with one cheap product.
- It's posted into fall deal season with Black Friday tags.
- For @brockstaaa's buyer the mechanic is identical. Swap "1200 mattress" for the salon blowout or the filler appointment the brief names. The anchor is what makes cheap feel smart.
