## 8 formats to own

### 1. The Reddit thread (own this one first)
Reddit is the most trusted "real people" look online, and nobody here uses it. A thread tells a story across slides on its own.
- **Slide 1:** a Reddit post in r/SkincareAddiction or r/beauty. "AITA for not telling my sister what I use on my skin?" with 14.2k upvotes and 2.1k comments. One line of story visible: "she's been asking for 3 months and honestly I'm just tired of everyone copying me…"
- **Slide 2:** the top comment, 9.8k upvotes: "NTA but you owe US the name 😭"
- **Slide 3:** OP's edit: "EDIT: ok since 400 of you asked, it's this. $18. please stop DMing me"
- **Slide 4:** the product on your vanity.
- **Why:** "EDIT: since everyone asked" is the most natural reveal there is. The upvote counts do the proof. It works for any product: skin, lips, hair, gummies.
- **Make it a series:** "Reddit made me buy it", one thread a day, different subreddits. Followers come back for the next one, and that's how you own a format.

### 2. "Someone messed up the price"
This is the 4M mechanic, and only one account has used it.
- **Slide 1:** a callout Facebook post with a deadline. "girls with thin lips don't forget, this week the lip plumper is literally pocket change".
- **Slide 2:** a screenshot of the product's real TikTok Shop listing with the flash sale price circled in red and "SOMEONE F*CKED UP.. 😳".
- **Slide 3:** a 3-item checklist over the product.
- **Rule:** only post it when the product is actually on a big discount. Check the listing that day. A fake "glitch" gets reported.

### 3. Identity callouts (the 4M + 703K formula, for your lane)
The two biggest Facebook posts named a specific person: "people with back pain", "blue collar men". Do the same with beauty identities nobody calls out:
- "girls with hooded eyes… stop fighting your mascara when this exists"
- "nurses who work 12s… your skin doesn't have to look like that after a night shift"
- "curly girls… stop sleeping on this"
- "girls with fine hair… stop paying $90 for a blowout when this exists"

Then the product, a 3-item checklist, and "(sorry about the price)". When someone sees their own exact identity, it stops them. Each callout is a new audience, and you can post one a day for months.

### 4. The lock screen
- **Slide 1:** an iPhone lock screen with 5 stacked notifications: "Mom: why does your hair look so good in the pics??", "Bestie: SEND ME WHAT YOU USE", "Him: ok you look different lately 👀", "Sister: hello?? answer me", "Instagram: 4 people commented on your post".
- **Slide 2:** "everyone texting me the same thing this week 😭"
- **Slide 3:** the product: "so here it is. you're welcome".
- **Why:** it's the most native-looking phone screenshot there is. Nobody posts it, and people stop on anything that looks like their own phone.

### 5. Google search
- **Slide 1:** a Google search bar typing "why does my hair look fried in every picture" with real-looking autocomplete suggestions under it.
- **Slide 2:** the "People also ask" box, with one answer expanded.
- **Slide 3:** the product: "found the answer. $14."
- **Why:** everyone's searched something embarrassing like this. It feels private, and that's why people swipe.

### 6. The misdirection text (the 1.37M "Plug" formula)
A text that reads like something scandalous, then it's a beauty product. Ideas:
- From "Him 🖤": "who did you get it from" / "the guy at work said he wants some too" / "i'm not sharing" / reveal: "it's a $12 lip oil 😭"
- From "Unknown": "you got the stuff?" / "yeah left it on the porch" / "you're a lifesaver" / reveal: the heatless curls headband.
- **Why:** curiosity, then the laugh, then the comments ("I thought it was going somewhere else 😭"). Keep it clean. The joke is the misdirection, not anything explicit.

### 7. The 1-star review that's a compliment
- **Slide 1:** a TikTok Shop review screenshot: "⭐ 1 star. my boyfriend won't stop touching my hair and I can't get anything done."
- **Slide 2:** another one: "⭐ 1 star. my sister stole it and I had to buy two."
- **Slide 3:** "the most dangerous $15 I ever spent."
- **Why:** people read reviews before they buy anyway, and a 1-star in yellow and red stops the eye. It's funny, and the fake complaints are the selling points.

### 8. Men's lane: the Facebook callout for guys
iluvdealz's "Blue collar men…" did 703K. Nobody's doing men's grooming or gadgets this way.
- "guys who work 12-hour shifts… stop going to bed with your feet killing you when these exist" (recovery slides or insoles)
- "men over 30… stop using 3-in-1 body wash on your face" (a men's skincare set, like the Based bundles that do 130K)
- "gym guys… your gym bag doesn't have to smell like that" (a shoe deodorizer)
- **Why:** guys buy fast and don't compare as much, and almost no one makes slideshows for them.

## How to set the trend (not just post it)

1. **Pick 2 formats and run them every day for 2 weeks:** Reddit (1) and identity callouts (3). Trends belong to whoever posts a format the most, the earliest.
2. **Name the series:** "Reddit made me buy it pt. 7". Numbered parts make people follow for the next one.
3. **Keep the screenshots perfect.** Real fonts, real UI and believable numbers (14.2k upvotes, not 1M). A sloppy fake kills it.
4. **Test each format on your best product first:** the lip plumper or the heatless curls. Then move the winner to new products.
5. **Track it on the Ore pull.** If it breaks out, put the same format on 3 more products that week.
