# PeptidePal: 8 ideas from the Oct 25 report

These are ideas only, not scripts. Edit, cut or combine whatever you want, then we'll build out the ones you keep. Each one names the winner it borrows from (the # is the ad's number in the report) and the gap it fills.

None of these repeat the three briefs we just made (second peptide, spreadsheet, cottage cheese).

---

## 1. "Sunday or Monday?" (cartoon sequel)

**Borrowed from:**
- **#7, our own fiber skit.** It's our only rising ad: 22 days live against our usual 7.
- **The report's "one countable moment" pattern.**

**The idea:**
- The same two cartoon women as #7, same style.
- One is about to take her shot and freezes: "Wait. Did I take it Sunday… or Monday?" The friend asks what her alarm says. "It went off. I swiped it." The friend: "So it reminded you. It just didn't write anything down."
- Then PeptidePal's week view, with Sunday checked off and the time it was done.

**Why it should work:**
- The cartoon format is the one thing of ours that's proven to run right now.
- "Sunday or Monday?" is the brief's biggest pain, and the report found no winner in the market using it.
- It's the "Reminder-Isn't-a-Record" mechanism, told as a scene and not explained.

**Format:** cartoon dialogue, about 45–60 seconds.

## 2. A week on a GLP-1, day by day

**Borrowed from:**
- **The day ladder:** Everly #26 ("Day 1… Day 3… Day 7… 2am") and #21 ("Week one… week four").
- **The market's shift into GLP-1 side effects:** Sculptique, Zafira, Jevawell.

**The idea:**
- A big "DAY 1" through "DAY 7" over each beat of her week after the shot:
  - Day 1: green
  - Day 2: not hungry at all
  - Day 3: half a yogurt
  - Day 5: fine
  - Day 7: starving and wondering why the scale stalled
- The turn: "I thought this was just how the shot feels. Turns out every bad day was predictable."
- The payoff: PeptidePal's graph shows the same week, with the peak days marked before they hit.

**Why it should work:**
- The day counter keeps people watching. Every winner over 60 seconds uses some version of it.
- Supplement brands are paying to sell a pill for these exact days. We're the only ones who can show the days coming.

**Format:** UGC or B-roll with big day-counter text, 45–60 seconds. It can also be a 7-panel static.

## 3. "This is not tracking."

**Borrowed from:**
- **Everly #13's scam-warning open** ("This is fake, this is fake, and this is definitely fake"). Sculptique copied it, and it's scaling.

**The idea:**
- A creator holds up her phone alarm: "This is not tracking." Then a Notes app list: "This is not tracking." Then a sticky note on the fridge: "And this is *definitely* not tracking."
- She explains why each one fails:
  - the alarm forgets the second you swipe it
  - Notes doesn't know what day it is
  - the sticky note can't count down a vial
- Then: "This is tracking." PeptidePal.

**Why it should work:**
- The open is a proven pattern interrupt from a 295-day winner.
- It blames the tool, not the viewer, which is what every long-runner in the report does.
- It also hits the "I can do this for free in Notes" objection head on.

**Format:** UGC, 25–35 seconds.

**Casting:** try a man in his 30s for one version. The report found no winner talking to under-40s or men, but they're 43% of our signups.

## 4. "My doctor asked what I've been doing. I handed her my phone."

**Borrowed from:**
- **The "my doctor brushed me off" pattern** in #13, #14, #15 and #21. Flipped around.

**The idea:**
- Every competitor sells the doctor who didn't listen. We sell the visit that went right: three months of doses, dates, side-effect notes and protein, in one screen she could just hand over.
- Open in the parking lot after the appointment: "That was the first time a doctor actually took me seriously."

**Why it should work:**
- The report found this half of the doctor story is completely open: "My doctors ask 'what have you been doing?'" and "this time they can show it" have no ad.
- It's a dream outcome, not a fear, which fits next to the three briefs that lean on fear.

**Format:** UGC, filmed in the car (like Caitlin's 58-day car selfie), 30–40 seconds.

## 5. "4 months of doses. Gone."

**Borrowed from:**
- **The report's gap list:** lost data is the brief's most painful app failure, and no ad touches it, competitors included.
- **The "one countable moment" pattern:** 4 months, one update, all gone.

**The idea:**
- A creator scrolls an empty log in another app: "I had four months of every dose in here. One update, and it's like I never started."
- Then why that's a big deal: she doesn't remember her doses, her site rotation, when her last vial started, or what she told her doctor.
- Then PeptidePal: her history stays put, and she can export it.

**Why it should work:**
- It names a fear direct competitors can't touch: their own reviews are full of it.
- It answers "why switch from the app I already use?", which none of our current ads do.

**Need to confirm:** what PeptidePal actually does to keep data safe (cloud backup, export) before we make this promise.

**Format:** UGC, 25–35 seconds.

## 6. Napkin math (the $300 mistake)

**Borrowed from:**
- **Our own reconstitution cheat card** (50 days live) and **the "$300 vial" meme** (47 days live). Both are still running, which proves this pain.
- **The crossed-out problem word** from Dulave's "PPI TRAP, BE GONE" (#16).

**The idea:**
- On-screen: "NAPKIN MATH", crossed out with a hand-drawn strike.
- A creator: "I mixed a $300 vial with math I did on the back of a receipt, and I was off by one decimal."
- How that plays out: every dose for a month is wrong, and she can't tell from how she feels.
- Then PeptidePal's calculator: put in the vial, it gives the units.

**Why it should work:**
- "I don't trust my own math" has no winner in the market. Only our own ads touch it, and they're two of our longest-running.

**Format:** UGC (25–30 seconds) plus a static version with the crossed-out "NAPKIN MATH" headline.

## 7. Customer quote statics

**Borrowed from:**
- **Testimonial-as-headline:** Dulave #17 ("My Stomach Has Never Felt Better", Jennifer Lawson, 47) and Everly #19 ("— Ugo, Age 62").
- **Many voices saying one result:** Spoiled Child #11 and #12.

**The idea:**
- A set of 3–5 statics. Each is a real PeptidePal App Store review in huge quote marks, with first name and age if we have them, over a phone showing the matching screen.
- A follow-up video could have 4–5 different creators each say their own line, cut together.

**Why it should work:**
- Every long-running supplement winner uses named people. None of our ads do. The report flagged this as the clearest thing competitors do that we don't.

**Need:** real PeptidePal reviews (we only quote real ones). Can Michael pull the best 10?

**Format:** statics first, cheap to test.

## 8. Steal the Everly skeleton

**Borrowed from:**
- **Everly #5**, a 295-day winner, and **Sculptique #6**, which copied it line for line onto a different product and has 13 versions in 24 days.

**The idea:**
- The proven skeleton with our nouns:
  1. "You have [deadline]. If you don't take advantage of this…"
  2. "Since using this, I…" with three specific things that changed (no more "Sunday or Monday", knows when the vial runs out, protein on peak days)
  3. "Don't miss this. I'm putting it right here."

**Why it should work:**
- The report found the skeleton is what carries over, not the product.
- No app ad in the market offers any deal or risk reversal, and the brief's loudest objection is about apps that "start free and then lock things."

**Need:** a real offer: a free trial, a discount, or a first month free. Without one, this idea doesn't work. What can PeptidePal offer?

**Format:** UGC, 30–45 seconds.
