# PeptidePal: 2 scripts for our aesthetic nurse creator

Iterations of "The Hair in Your Hand" and "When Your Food Noise Comes Back," rewritten so she speaks as the expert. She doesn't say her name.

**Before filming:** she only says the [bracketed] lines if they're true for her. Every "my clients" line has to be something she actually sees or hears. If it isn't, use the swap next to it. No doses, timing instructions or "what to take." She explains why it happens, and the app does the tracking.

## 2. When Your Food Noise Comes Back (nurse version)

**Funnel stage:** TOFU. Problem aware: people microdosing who aren't perfect with their schedule and think the food noise coming back is their fault.

**Why we're doing it:**
- **The original brief's case still stands.** Noom's #1 ad is "Microdose GLP-1s starting at $79," and about 1 in 7 people on GLP-1 shots have tried microdosing. Real comments say "the food noise came back hard," and almost nobody takes their shot on the exact same day every week.
- **She fixes the brief's weak spot.** Michael said the "a microdose wears off sooner" part wasn't clear enough. A nurse explaining it makes it believable, and "it's not your willpower" from a clinician lands harder than from a peer.
- **Med spas are where a lot of microdosing happens,** so an aesthetic nurse is a natural person to hear it from.

**Script**

| Time | They say | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0-6s | "I'm an aesthetic nurse, and if you're microdosing your GLP-1 and your food noise keeps coming back, I promise it's probably not your willpower." | Text hook: Nurse here: how to keep it away |
| 6-14s | "[I hear this all the time.] 'I take my shot on Sunday, I'm fine Monday, Tuesday, and then some weeks, Friday night, I'm standing in front of the fridge like I never started.'" | |
| 14-22s | "And people are so hard on themselves. Like, why can't I just stop eating? I'm paying for this every month, I lose a few pounds and gain it right back. Some of them are ready to quit." | |
| 22-30s | "But when you actually look at it, there's almost always a pattern. It's not every week. It's the weeks they took it late, or early, or skipped one 'cause life got crazy." | |
| 30-40s | "Here's why. Your shot doesn't stay at the same level all week. It's strongest a day or two after you take it, and then it slowly wears off. And on a microdose, you're starting with less, so it wears off a lot sooner. There's way less room for error." | |
