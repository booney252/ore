# PeptidePal briefs, week of 2026-10-16

_Built from 30 ads (5 own)._

## What's winning this week

**1. A real moment + one search-style line of text, product on screen by second 2.** Cal AI's top ad (2922821464729858, score 78: running 135 days, still active, 2 versions) is 9 seconds, no talking: a home-cooked pan of fajitas, the caption "best way to track calories?", the app opens at 2.3s, and the dashboard fills in. They're already iterating it ("Please stop eyeballing your calories", 1679590623726788, 2 versions). Cheap to make, easy to version, and it reads like a recommendation, not an ad.

**2. "Is anyone else…?" callout, then a reframe: the shot is the easy part, everything after is the hard part.** Shotsy's two longest-running talking heads use the same creator and the same move (1561391218871311, score 70: running 81 days, still active; 1213436354123385, ran 111 days): an expressive, worried face, a question that lets the viewer self-select in 2 seconds, then "the real challenge is everything after that." 1189211106725325 (ran 128 days) opens on a Notes app titled "Nausea?" with "Yes / ? / ?" — the old way, shown, not described.

**3. Show the old-way mess.** MyFitnessPal's before/after (1549827920488430, 56 days, still active) cuts to a Notes-app "August Food Log" full of "300ish??" guesses. Our own two best ads are both this pattern: the "WITHOUT / WITH PEPTIDEPAL" split (38322681290713842, score 46: running 38 days, still active) and the "$300 vial" hamster meme over handwritten unit math (3627760524040144, score 49: running 38 days, still active, 2 versions). This is our proven lane.

**4. A pattern you have to watch to the end.** MyFitnessPal's "This is 150 calories" (916912974809352, 56 days, still active): one line repeated over six plates, then the reveal, then a joke. Watch time does the work.

**5. Good / better / best, reshot with several creators.** Pep AI is running the same "GOOD MyFitnessPal / BETTER Cal AI / BEST Pep AI" script with at least two creators (2307406043393131, 1835767747774192). Too new to call a winner, but reshooting one script with new faces is how brands scale what works. Worth watching.

**Our own ads vs competitors.** Our winners are the right *angle* (chaos → one screen), but most of them break the research brief's hard rules for paid Meta: the "$300 vial" meme and the split-screen both show a syringe graphic and unit/mcg math; the protein cheat sheet (3693610030793473) names "GLP-1" and medication half-life; the animated nutrients ad (1769747397682465) says "so the weight you lose is the weight you meant to lose"; the "Nauseous on your protocol?" ad says "peptide" on screen. These are disapproval and account risks, separate from performance. The briefs below keep what's working and remove what's banned.

**Gaps nobody's ads touch (from the research brief):**
- **The second-compound moment.** The brief calls it *the* buying moment ("The day they add a second vial, the math, the schedule and the fridge all break"). No competitor ad addresses it.
- **Doctor as ally / "show your doctor."** No one uses it; 9 in 10 peptide-aware Americans say a physician's involvement raises their trust.
- **Our actual buyer on camera.** Almost every winning creator is 20-35. Only Pep AI (28601978322796138) shows a woman in her 40s. The Midlife Woman (38-62) is barely represented.
- **"I'm not a tech person."** Every competitor sells features; nobody sells "simple enough."

### Brief 1: "How in the world do you keep track?" — kitchen-counter demo
- **Type:** iteration of 2922821464729858 + 1679590623726788 (Cal AI)
- **Built from:** Cal AI's top ad (score 78: running 135 days, still active, 2 versions) and its newer variant (score 41: running 24 days, still active, 2 versions). What's winning: a real, unglamorous home moment + one line of text that sounds like something you'd type into search, product by second 2, payoff by second 6, no talking. We swap their question for our buyer's.
- **Segment + awareness stage:** The Midlife Woman. Problem Aware.
- **Angle:** The routine you're juggling on sticky notes, in one place, in one glance.
- **Hooks (3 options):**
  1. Text: "how in the world do you keep track of everything?" — First frame: a fridge door covered in sticky notes and a paper calendar with handwritten checkmarks, kitchen morning light.
  2. Text: "is there an app for this or is everyone just using sticky notes" — First frame: hand pulling a sticky note off the fridge that says "Sun or Mon??"
  3. Text: "please tell me I'm not the only one tracking this on the fridge" — First frame: same fridge, woman's hand (40s-50s, wedding ring, no manicure glam) hovering over the notes.
- **Format:** silent POV demo, 9-12s, 0.4 cuts/sec (4-5 cuts), trending low-key audio.
- **Beats:**
  - 0-2s: fridge of sticky notes + text hook
  - 2-3s: hand picks up phone from the counter, PeptidePal opens
  - 3-7s: Today screen: what's due today, what was taken yesterday, where she is in the week (neutral demo account, no compound names)
  - 7-9s: quick tap to log lunch, protein ring fills
  - 9-11s: she pulls the sticky notes off the fridge and drops them in the trash; end card with app icon
- **On-screen text:** hook line (above); end card: "Your whole routine. One calm screen."
- **CTA:** end card "Download PeptidePal". Button: Download.
- **Customer language to use:** "How in the world do you keep track of everything?" (hook 1, from the research brief, as a question, not a testimonial); "one calm screen"; "know exactly where you are this week."
- **Creator notes:** shoot on a real fridge in a real kitchen, phone-held, natural light. Hands of a woman 40-60. Demo account must show no compound or drug names, no doses in mg/mcg, no syringe or calculator screens. Make 3-4 versions by swapping only the hook text, like Cal AI does.
- **Compliance check:** No claims. "One calm screen" and "know where you are this week" are functional promises the app can deliver. Confirm the demo account shows no compound names or unit math. Passes every hard rule.
