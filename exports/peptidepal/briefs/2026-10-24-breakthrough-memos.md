# PeptidePal: breakthrough memos for the Oct 24 briefs

These memos explain the why behind each of the three approved UGC briefs:
- **The insight:** what we found.
- **Why it moves people:** why it should change what the buyer does.
- **Why now:** why the creative should win.
- **What we learn:** what the result tells us either way.

They draw on the research brief, the chosen unique mechanisms, the Oct 21 Winning Ads Report and Caitlin's ad.

## The big idea behind all three

PeptidePal's buyer isn't failing at peptides. She's failing at being the glue between five tools:
- alarms
- Notes
- a spreadsheet
- a calorie app
- her memory

Every failure story in the research is a tracking or math failure, and nobody admits it ("nobody brags about their spreadsheet"). Most of the market is **problem aware**: she feels the mess but doesn't know an app would fix it.

So none of these ads sell features. Each one names **one specific moment where her system breaks** and explains *why* it breaks, so the app reads as the obvious fix and not as one more tool.

All three borrow the same proven shell from **Caitlin's ad** (live 56 days, PeptidePal's strongest own ad):
1. a protective warning given on her behalf
2. a physical interrupt
3. the problem dug into
4. the app arriving late

The one ad we know failed, "Monday dose still doing on Thursday", opened on the explanation. These ads open on the warning and earn the explanation.

## Brief 1: Before You Add a Second Peptide

**The breakthrough:** Adding the second peptide is the moment her system stops working. That's the **Second-Compound Cliff**. She can't feel or see how much is still in her body, so she's guessing, and the side effects she can't explain come from that guess.

**Why it moves her:**
- The research names one trigger moment for buying: *"The day they add a second vial, the math, the schedule and the fridge all break."*
- A once-a-week pen is easy to remember. Pen plus vial plus an every-other-day dose breaks memory, alarms and Notes.
- 61% call themselves new or beginners. They've never had half-life explained.
- The coffee-at-3pm picture makes it click in one listen. Then "you're stacking it on top of what's still in there" turns a concept into a fear she can feel.

**The belief shift:** from "I'll just keep track of it" to "I literally can't see this, and the app can."

**Why this creative:**
- Caitlin's warning frame: *"till somebody explains this to you, 'cause nobody explained it to me"*.
- Michael's half-life teaching, with the hidden cause explained before the product.
- This is the only one of the three that teaches half-life, on purpose.

**What we learn:**
- **If it wins:** *education is the hook* for this market. Teaching a hidden cause beats naming a pain, and we should build more "nobody explained this" ads.
- **If it loses while Brief 3 wins:** the cause has to be something she can see (her arms), not something invisible (blood levels).

**Funnel:** TOFU, the widest of the three. It also works on a one-shot viewer, because she learns her shot lingers.

## Brief 2: The New Vial Night

**The breakthrough:** Her spreadsheet isn't bad. It's a **second job**. She built it in a whole Sunday, it works, and it breaks every time something changes:
- a new vial
- a dose going up
- a second product

It also just sits there. It doesn't remind her, doesn't know she's running out, and doesn't notice anything.

**Why it moves her:**
- The top objection in the research is *"I can do this for free in Notes, a calendar or a spreadsheet."* This ad doesn't fight that objection; it agrees with it.
  - It opens by granting that her system works: *"my dose math's great, my protein list's great"*.
  - Then it shows the one night it stops working.
- Users already say this in their own reviews: "I actually stick with it instead of forgetting to update a spreadsheet", "replaced my notes app and a spreadsheet".
- She's proud of her spreadsheet. Calling it stupid loses her. Calling it a second job she never signed up for wins her, because it's true and slightly funny.

**The belief shift:** from "my system's fine" to "my system is a chore I redo forever."

**Why this creative:**
- Everly's (report #12) concede-first turn.
- The report's "you already tried the usual fix, here's why it fails" pattern.
- One countable, slightly embarrassing moment: fixing formulas at 11pm.
- **No half-life**, on the owner's call. This ad is pure frustration, so each script teaches one lesson at most.

**What we learn:**
- **If it wins:** the *competitor is the spreadsheet,* not other apps. We should make more "your free workaround is costing you" ads (Notes, alarms, the paper log).
- **If it loses:** the spreadsheet crowd may be smaller or more stubborn than the alarm crowd. Test the Reminder-Isn't-a-Record angle next.

**Funnel:** MOFU. She's already organized enough to build a spreadsheet, so she's closer to buying.

## Brief 3: Cottage Cheese at 9pm

**The breakthrough:** The calorie app she trusts is cheering her toward the wrong outcome. That's the **Low-Appetite Protein Blind Spot**.
- On the days her shot peaks, her appetite shuts off. She eats a yogurt and the app gives her a gold star.
- But low protein on those days means her body eats muscle. Losing muscle is what makes the weight come back as fat when she comes off the shot.
- The calorie app was built to help her eat *less*. It can't see her peak days.

**Why it moves her:**
- Women over-index on weight loss (88%). The calorie app is the tool she already has open, so this ad goes after the tool she trusts most.
- The research's protein pain is real and in her own words:
  - "If I could make Protein the first thing I see instead of calories, I'd give you 10 stars"
  - "I write down what my protein is and then do math"
- This is the one ad that **pokes the bear**. It goes from "look at your arms" to the birthday photo, "deflated", "it was muscle", and "it comes back as fat".
  - That's a fear she can check on herself right now. Being told she looks great while losing muscle is the hidden cost nobody warned her about.

**The belief shift:** from "I'm doing great, my app says so" to "my app can't see the days that matter, and they're hurting me."

**Why this creative:**
- Caitlin's warning with a physical interrupt.
- The report's "one tool, one flaw" idea (report #15, cut down to a single enemy).
- One concrete moment: a sad cup of cottage cheese at 9pm.
- PeptidePal's own #6 and Caitlin's ad both prove the late reveal works.

**What we learn:**
- **If it wins:** *visible, body-level stakes* beat logistics. We'd make more "the hidden cost you can see in the mirror" ads, and probably move protein to the front of PeptidePal's positioning for women.
- **If it loses:** body-image fear may need a gentler entry, so test the B hook (the open loop).

**Funnel:** MOFU. It's for the woman already running a calorie app plus alarms, the most common setup among buyers who are just starting to look for an app.

## How the three fit together

Each one hits a different breaking point and teaches at most one thing:

| Brief | Breaking point | Lesson | Enemy | Emotion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Adding the second peptide | Half-life and stacking | Guessing | "I can't see this" |
| 2 | A new vial or dose change | None | The spreadsheet | "I gave myself a second job" |
| 3 | Peak days | Low protein eats muscle | The calorie app | "It's been hurting me" |

Run as a set, the three tell us which lever moves this buyer most: **invisible risk, wasted effort, or visible body cost.** We should double down on whichever wins next.
