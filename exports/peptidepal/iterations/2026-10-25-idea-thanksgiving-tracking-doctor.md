# PeptidePal iterations: UGC video

**Your idea:** Three ideas picked from the Oct 25 report ideas, rewritten Oct 26 with the owner's feedback:
- **Thanksgiving:** a very digestible half-life breakdown, no leftovers metaphor. If you don't get it, you can have more in your system on Thanksgiving than you think and not touch a piece of food, and it explains the side effects you "randomly" get.
- **"This is not tracking":** built on the owner's own write. The "random" side effects aren't random. Mention the solution early without naming it, so the viewer wants to know what it is while it's being explained.
- **The doctor visit:** a personal storytime. "I just went to the doctor for the first time since starting peptides, and you might want to hear this before you have yours."

_Built with winners #7, #13, #14, #15 and #21 of the Winning Ads Report of 2026-10-25, the gold standard briefs, and PeptidePal's feature list in brand.md._

### Iteration 1: The Awkward Thanksgiving

**Funnel stage: TOFU.** It calls out every GLP-1 user with a date they all have coming. Of the three, only this one explains half-life step by step.

- **Built on:**
  - **The owner's hook,** word for word.
  - **#7, our fiber skit** (22 days live against our usual 7): "You tried to have tacos on your peak day."
  - **The gold standard's half-life approach:** say what it means in one plain sentence, then what it does to you. No metaphor this time, just the plain mechanics with a Monday-to-Monday example.
  - **The app:** an estimate of what's still on board from your logged doses, peak alerts, and prep for the worst days.

- **Why it should work for us:**
  - Thanksgiving is Thursday, Nov 26, and every GLP-1 user has it on the calendar.
  - It makes half-life matter in two ways the viewer feels: why you won't touch your food on Thanksgiving, and why your side effects feel "random."
  - The "random" side effects line turns a confusing experience into something with a cause. That's the report's #1 pattern: it isn't your fault, nobody showed you.

- **Setup:**
  - **Creator:** a woman, 40-55, warm and a little funny, in a sweater.
  - **Place:** her kitchen or dining table, with a pie or a candle in the background.
  - **Shot:** phone propped at eye level, with one cutaway to the Today screen and the peak alert.
  - **Length:** target 45-50s. Run Nov 1-25.
  - **Brief to the creator:** say it in your own words and keep the beats in order. No compound names. If you use a number for how long your shot takes to drop to half, use "about a week."

- **Alternate hooks (2):**
  1. **They say:** "Don't be the only one at Thanksgiving dinner not eating."
     **First frame:** she sets down a plate with one bite of turkey on it. On screen: "THANKSGIVING ON A GLP-1 🦃"
  2. **They say:** "My aunt asked if I was sick. No, Linda, it's my peak day."
     **First frame:** she's mid-eye-roll, a fork in her hand. On screen: "IT'S MY PEAK DAY, LINDA"

### Iteration 2: This Is Not Tracking

**Funnel stage: MOFU.** For the viewer who thinks they already track, with alarms, Notes and a sticky note on the fridge.

The turn is the owner's: the "random" side effects were caused by what these methods couldn't show. The solution is mentioned early without a name ("the app that finally showed me"), so the viewer wants to know what it is while it's being explained.

- **Built on:**
  - **The owner's write,** kept close to how it was said.
  - **Everly #13's scam-warning open** ("This is fake, this is fake, and this is definitely fake"), which Sculptique copied and is scaling.
  - **The report's #1 pattern:** blame the tool, never the buyer.
  - **The open loop:** the solution is promised at 8s and named at 30s. Everything in between is the proof of why it works, so the viewer is checking each beat against the thing they're waiting to see.

- **Why it should work for us:**
  - It answers the brief's top objection ("I can do this for free in Notes") by showing what free tools *can't* see.
  - "My side effects aren't random" is a bigger reason to switch than "I forgot a dose." It's about how you feel every week.
  - It uses the peaks only to explain the side effects. Iteration 1 teaches half-life step by step; this one doesn't.

- **Setup:**
  - **Creator:** shoot two versions. Version A is a woman, 40-55. Version B is a man, 30-40: men are 43% of signups and no winner in the report talks to them.
  - **Place:** the kitchen, near the fridge.
  - **Props:** a phone with a dismissed alarm, a phone with a Notes list, a sticky note on the fridge.
  - **Shot:** handheld for the three-prop open, then propped. Keep the phone face down on the counter from 8s to 30s, then flip it over for the reveal.
  - **Length:** target 35-40s.

- **Alternate hooks (2):**
  1. **They say:** "My nausea wasn't random, and it took me two months and a different app to figure that out."
     **First frame:** she's leaning on the counter, phone face down in front of her. On screen: "it wasn't random 🤢"
  2. **They say:** "If you track your shots with an alarm and a note on your phone, your side effects are probably not as random as you think."
     **First frame:** she holds the alarm phone up to the lens. On screen: "⚠️ THIS ISN'T TRACKING ⚠️"

### Iteration 3: Before Your First Doctor Visit

**Funnel stage: MOFU.** A personal storytime: a warning to people who haven't had the visit yet, built on the owner's hook.

It sells the dream outcome, the appointment that works, by showing the one that didn't. It flips the "my doctor brushed me off" pattern: here nobody's the villain except the guessing.

- **Built on:**
  - **The owner's hook,** close to word for word.
  - **The report's "my doctor brushed me off" pattern** (#13, #14, #15, #21), turned into a doctor who couldn't help because there was nothing to go on.
  - **A gap the report found:** "My doctors ask 'what have you been doing?'" has no ad.
  - **The "one countable moment" pattern:** a 20-minute appointment, a second copay.
  - **Caitlin's car selfie** (58 days live): same setting, same honest register.
  - **The app:** full history you can scroll and show your doctor, notes on any dose, protein on the same screen.

- **Why it should work for us:**
  - Storytime holds attention through a 50-second video, which our explainers didn't (they stopped at 18-38 days).
  - The cost is concrete: a wasted appointment and paying for a second one.
  - It gives a reason to track that isn't about the app: getting real help from your doctor.

- **Setup:**
  - **Creator:** a woman, 40-55, real and unpolished.
  - **Place:** her car in the parking lot after an appointment, seatbelt on, phone propped on the dash.
  - **Shot:** one take, with one cutaway to scrolling the history.
  - **Length:** target 45-55s. Storytime can run longer.
  - **Brief to the creator:** if this happened to you, tell your version. Use your real details (how long the visit was, what it cost). No compound names, and don't say what the doctor changed.

- **Alternate hooks (2):**
  1. **They say:** "My doctor asked when I went up a dose, and I said, like, a month ago? And she just looked at me."
     **First frame:** in the car, she half-laughs into the camera. On screen: "when your doctor asks 'when did you go up?'"
  2. **They say:** "I paid for two doctor visits when I only needed one, so learn from me."
     **First frame:** she holds up two receipts. On screen: "learn from me 🙃"
