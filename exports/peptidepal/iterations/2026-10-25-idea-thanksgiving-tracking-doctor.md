### Iteration 1: The Awkward Thanksgiving

**Funnel stage: TOFU.** It calls out every GLP-1 user with a date they all have coming. Only this one of the three teaches half-life, because Thanksgiving gives it a reason to matter.

- **Built on:**
  - **Your hook,** word for word.
  - **#7, our fiber skit** (22 days live against our usual 7): "You tried to have tacos on your peak day." This turns that joke into the whole ad.
  - **The report's seasonal finding:** time-pegged hooks keep converting (Spoiled Child's "LEAVE THE PUFFINESS IN 2025" is still running).
  - **The gold standard's half-life approach:** one picture, then what it means for you. Here the picture is the leftovers, so we don't reuse the coffee line.
  - **The app feature:** peak alerts and prep. From your logged doses it shows where your levels are, alerts you when things are peaking, and tells you how to get ready (water beforehand, protein before appetite drops).

- **Why it should work for us:**
  - Thanksgiving is Thursday, Nov 26. Every GLP-1 user has it on the calendar, and nobody has thought about it in terms of their shot.
  - The moment it describes is real and slightly embarrassing: everyone on seconds, you pushing turkey around, an aunt asking if you're sick. The report found every long-runner is built on a moment like this.
  - Half-life finally has a reason to matter to the viewer. It's the thing that decides which day you can't eat.

- **Setup:**
  - **Creator:** a woman, 40-55, warm and a little funny, in a sweater.
  - **Place:** her kitchen or dining table, with a few fall touches (a pie, a candle). Nothing staged.
  - **Shot:** phone propped at eye level, with one cutaway to a screen recording of the Today screen and the peak alert.
  - **Length:** target 40-45s. Run Nov 1-25.
  - **Brief to the creator:** say it in your own words, keep the beats in order, and keep the leftovers picture. No compound names.

- **Alternate hooks (2):**
  1. **They say:** "Don't be the only one at Thanksgiving dinner not eating."
     **First frame:** she sets a plate with one bite of turkey on it down in front of the camera. On screen: "THANKSGIVING ON A GLP-1 🦃"
  2. **They say:** "My aunt asked if I was sick. No, Linda, it's my peak day."
     **First frame:** she's mid-eye-roll, a fork in hand. On screen: "IT'S MY PEAK DAY, LINDA"

### Iteration 2: This Is Not Tracking

**Funnel stage: MOFU.** For the viewer who already "tracks," with alarms, Notes and a sticky note on the fridge. It teaches nothing. It shows why each tool fails, then the one that doesn't.

- **Built on:**
  - **Everly #13's scam-warning opening** ("This is fake, this is fake, and this is definitely fake"). Sculptique copied it and it's scaling.
  - **The report's #1 pattern:** blame the tool, never the buyer ("That's why yours did nothing").
  - **The chosen mechanism, the Reminder-Isn't-a-Record Gap:** the alarm knows *when*, not *whether*.
  - **Gold standard Brief 2's voice:** frustration with the old system, a little funny, no lecture.

- **Why it should work for us:**
  - It answers the brief's top objection ("I can do this for free in Notes, a calendar or a spreadsheet") by holding up each free tool and showing where it breaks.
  - "Did I take it Sunday or Monday?" is the brief's biggest pain, and the report found no winner using it.
  - The props make the hook visual with the sound off.

- **Setup:**
  - **Creator:** shoot two versions. Version A is a woman, 40-55. Version B is a man, 30-40: men are 43% of signups and no winner in the report talks to them.
  - **Place:** the kitchen, near the fridge (the sticky note lives there).
  - **Props:** a phone showing a dismissed alarm, a phone showing a Notes list, a sticky note on the fridge reading "SHOT SUN / vial #2 ??".
  - **Shot:** a handheld selfie for the three-prop open, then propped for the rest. One cutaway to the app.
  - **Length:** target 30-35s.

- **Alternate hooks (2):**
  1. **They say:** "If your whole protocol is an alarm and a note on your phone, I have bad news."
     **First frame:** she holds up the phone with the alarm. On screen: "⚠️ THIS ISN'T TRACKING ⚠️"
  2. **They say:** "I thought I was tracking my shots for five months, and I was really just setting alarms."
     **First frame:** a close-up of a swiped-away alarm notification.

- **Script:**

| Time | They say | On screen |

| 5-9s | "I ran my whole protocol on these three for like five months and honestly thought I was doing great." | She leans on the counter. Text: "5 months of this 😅" |

### Iteration 3: She Just Looked at Me

**Funnel stage: MOFU.** It sells the dream outcome, the appointment that goes right, where the other two lean on a problem. It flips the "my doctor brushed me off" pattern every supplement long-runner uses.

- **Built on:**
  - **The report's "my doctor brushed me off" pattern** (#13, #14, #15, #21), turned around. Here the doctor isn't the villain. The guessing is.
  - **A gap the report found:** "My doctors ask 'what have you been doing?'" and "this time they can show it" have no ad.
  - **Caitlin's car selfie** (58 days live, our longest-running): same setting, same honest register.
  - **The app features:** full history you can scroll and search and show your doctor, notes on any dose (how you felt, nausea), protein on the same screen.

- **Why it should work for us:**
  - Every GLP-1 user has had the appointment where they can't answer the questions. It's embarrassing in a way people recognize right away.
  - It gives a reason to track that isn't about the app: getting help from your doctor.
  - It ends on relief, the emotional shape every long-runner in the report ends on.

- **Setup:**
  - **Creator:** a woman, 45-55, real and unpolished.
  - **Place:** her car in the parking lot, right after an appointment, seatbelt on and phone propped on the dash. This is the Caitlin look.
  - **Shot:** one take, with one cutaway to scrolling the history screen.
  - **Length:** target 35-40s.
  - **Brief to the creator:** if you've had this appointment, tell your version. No compound names, and don't say what the doctor changed.

- **Alternate hooks (2):**
  1. **They say:** "My doctor stopped mid-sentence and asked, 'Wait, can you send me that?'"
     **First frame:** in the car, she holds her phone up to the lens showing the history screen. On screen: "what my doctor said 👀"
  2. **They say:** "I used to walk into my doctor's office and kind of make up half my answers."
     **First frame:** she exhales and leans back on the headrest. On screen: "me at every appointment 🙃"

- **Script:**
