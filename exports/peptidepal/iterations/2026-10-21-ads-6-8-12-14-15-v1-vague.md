### Iteration 1: I'm Asking Because I Love You

- **From:** ad #6 (PeptidePal own ad, 1114026504421349). Grade B (rising): running 18 days and still active, when most PeptidePal ads stop by day 8.
- **Type:** 90/10.
- **What we're borrowing:**
  - The hook, word for word: "I'm asking because I love you."
  - The countable, slightly embarrassing detail ("You've been in there 40 minutes" becomes "since nine o'clock").
  - The pattern reveal: "Every week?" / "Every week."
  - The question that exposes what she's missing: "Are you tracking your fiber?" / "My what?" becomes "Are you keeping a record?" / "A what?"
  - The friend-as-guide product entry around 30s, and the phone-handover close.
  - We drop the fiber feature, the peak-day curve and the bathroom context. All three break brand rules.
  - Format change: one creator plays the friend in POV and repeats the viewer's answers back.
- **Why it should work for us:**
  - Segment and pain: the Midlife Woman, Problem Aware. The pain is the Reminder-Isn't-a-Record Gap: "A phone alarm tells you *when*… never *whether*."
  - It names her exact workaround ("over a third rely on alarms") without calling her system stupid.
  - The stake passes the one-weekly-item test because it's half-life. Then the second item stacks a second half-life on top.
  - Objection 11 ("just another company selling me something") is handled the way the original handled it: the app arrives as a sister's advice.
  - It closes on the mixed routine plus food, so it never sells a bare log.
- **Setup:**
  - Who: woman, 48-56, soft knit top, reading glasses pushed up.
  - Where: leaning in a kitchen doorway at night, warm lamp light.
  - How it's shot: phone propped at couch height, so she's looking down at "you" on the sofa. One cut to her sitting beside the camera holding her phone.
  - Length: about 52s.
- **Alternate hooks (2):**
  1. "Did you take it, or did you just turn the alarm off?" First frame: she holds her own phone to the lens showing a swiped-away alarm notification, eyebrows up.
  2. "You've been staring at that calendar for five minutes. What are you looking for?" First frame: she leans over the camera as if reading over the viewer's shoulder.
- **Script:**

- **Brand rules check:**
  - No "shot," drug or injection words. "Your weekly one" stands in for rules.md's "your weekly shot." If compliance clears it, "your weekly shot" can replace it.
  - Half-life appears only as the reason to track. There are no intervals and no timing advice.
  - The protein line is Michael's reason, rephrased without "GLP" or "fat." It's framed as why to watch the number, not a promised result.
  - The reminder is mentioned only alongside the log, per the brand's mechanism.

### Iteration 2: Four Apps, One Job

- **From:** ad #8 (Pep AI, 28601978322796138). Grade B (rising): running 16 days and still active, when most Pep AI ads stop by day 7.
- **Type:** Pattern transfer.
- **What we're borrowing:**
  - A buyer-age woman with the real app on screen from frame 0.
  - The fast "it tracks… it tracks…" feature run that ends "This app is everything you need, all apps in one."
  - We swap the banned "If you're taking peptides" callout for a hook built on her own phone. Every list item gets its why, so the list reads as consequences rather than features.
- **Why it should work for us:**
  - Segment: the 35-44 group, the biggest signup bracket, and a woman. She's Just Starting to Look: "Usually runs a calorie app for food plus a phone reminder for doses."
  - Mechanism: Split-Screen Blindness. "Food alone misses half the picture."
  - Pain: "My routine lives in five places." It uses the brief's language: "replaced my notes app and a spreadsheet."
  - It's MOFU, so it names the tools she already uses (calorie app, calendar, spreadsheet) and shows what each one can't see.
- **Setup:**
  - Who: woman, about 40-46, casual tee, hair up.
  - Where: her kitchen counter at lunch, with a half-eaten bowl on the counter.
  - How it's shot: handheld selfie with her own phone held beside her face, screen to the lens, from frame 0. Quick jump cuts on each item.
  - Length: about 42s.
- **Alternate hooks (2):**
  1. "Count the apps I opened before lunch." First frame: phone to the lens, her thumb flicking from calendar to spreadsheet to calorie app.
  2. "My food was in one app. My schedule was in my head." First frame: she holds two phones side by side, one in each hand.
- **Script:**

| Time | They say | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0-3s | I had four apps doing one job. This is the one I kept. | Woman at the counter, phone beside her face, PeptidePal Today screen visible. Text: "4 apps → 1" |
| 3-8s | Calorie app had my food. Calendar had my alarms. Spreadsheet had what I took. The math was in my head. | Jump cut on each tool. She ticks them off on her fingers. Text: "food / alarms / log / math" |
| 8-12s | None of them could see each other. I'd log lunch and have no idea if today was even a dose day. | Text: "None of them talk" |
| 12-17s | So now, this. Weekly one, daily one, the every-other-day one. One schedule, each on its own rhythm. | She turns the phone fully to the lens and scrolls the week view. |
| 17-22s | Why I care: the day matters. Half-life. With two going, they overlap, and I want to know exactly where each one is. | Text: "know exactly where you are this week" |
| 22-25s | I tap it when I do it. It keeps what I took and when. | Screen: check-off with a timestamp. |
| 25-30s | Units, I put in what I've got and it does the math. It doesn't tell me what to take. It just does the math on my plan. | Screen: unit entry. Text: "units, done right" |
| 30-36s | Food's right under it. Calories, protein. Protein's the one I watch, because that's what makes all this work, and it's how I keep my muscle. | Screen: she logs her lunch in a few taps and the protein number moves. |
| 36-39s | Same screen. Same day. First time I've seen my whole week as one picture. | Screen: week view with check-ins and meals on the same days. |
| 39-42s | Everything I was juggling, one app. PeptidePal. Make sure it's this icon. | She taps the icon on her home screen to the lens. Text: "PeptidePal ↓ Download" |

- **CTA:** "Make sure it's this icon." Button: **Download**, linked to the correct listing.
- **Brand rules check:**
  - No "#1," peptide names, vial icons or injection-site tracking, all of which appear in the original.
  - The unit math line states only that it calculates on what she enters. It never says what to take.
  - Competitor apps are described by type, not name.
  - The protein why follows Michael's reason without "GLP" or "fat."

### Iteration 3: Not That One

- **From:** ad #12 (Everly, 1909400266367189). Grade A: running 223 days, still active, 3 versions.
- **Type:** 90/10.
- **What we're borrowing:**
  - The rule-of-three warning hook: "This is fake, this is fake, and this is definitely fake" becomes "This isn't it. This isn't it. And this is definitely not it."
  - The persistent top banner "2026 Scam to watch for‼️" becomes "2026 app mix-up to watch for‼️".
  - The concession: "I know the bags look exactly the same, but what they put inside, very different."
  - A countable self-recognition checklist with an age qualifier.
  - The prior-failure line: "If you've tried other brands and nothing changed."
  - "Don't take my word for it, verify it yourself."
  - Bold lime-box captions, 1-3 words at a time.
  - We never call other apps fake or scams. They're just "not it."
- **Why it should work for us:**
  - It turns a real objection into the hook: "Is this even the right app? I searched and found a dead one." The brief says at least four App Store listings use PeptidePal or PeptiPal names.
  - The warning frame reads as help rather than a pitch, which suits a buyer who distrusts ads.
  - Segment: the 55+ woman, who over-indexes among payers. She's Product Aware, often the friend others copy ("the one who figured it out").
  - It's BOFU, so it goes straight to what's inside and lands on rule 12: link to the correct listing.
- **Setup:**
  - Who: woman, 56-62, long silver hair, distinctive glasses or bold earrings.
  - Where: dining table, daylight, lived-in home.
  - How it's shot: phone propped at eye level, medium close-up. She holds her phone to the lens in nearly every shot. Hard cuts every 3-4s.
  - Length: about 56s.
- **Alternate hooks (2):**
  1. "Search it and you'll find at least four. Here's the real one." First frame: her phone to the lens, App Store results scrolling, other listings blurred.
  2. "Before you download the app your friend told you about, check the icon." First frame: the PeptidePal icon held big to the lens, her face half out of frame.

### Iteration 4: Okay, I'm Back

- **From:** ad #14 (Kilgour MD, 792339897068477). Grade A: running 175 days, still active, 4 versions.
- **Type:** Pattern transfer.
- **What we're borrowing:**
  - The native restart open with the product shoved at the lens at 0s: "Okay, I'm back. Let me get this set up for you real quick."
  - The price objection named in his own voice: "Yes, it is a little pricey and I get it. I felt the sting too."
  - The skeptic confession: "I go through every single review."
  - The sunk-cost tally of money already wasted.
  - The close as a choice.
  - We don't copy the before-and-after, the guarantee or the clinician claims. PeptidePal has none of those.
- **Why it should work for us:**
  - Segment: the Fix-It Man. Men pay at 19.8%, and payers skew older.
  - Mechanism: the Hidden Mistake Tax.
  - The buyer pays cash and watches every dollar: "The only harm I've experienced has been to my wallet." He's already paying for his routine, so the price objection flips into "don't pay twice for a mix-up."
  - Michael's authority hook (tenure) gives the narrator a reason to be trusted.
  - It's MOFU, so it names what he already tried (calendar, spreadsheet, a tracker that couldn't hold everything) before showing the fix.
- **Setup:**
  - Who: man, 52-60, flannel or work tee, gray stubble.
  - Where: kitchen table or workbench in the garage.
  - How it's shot: the open is handheld and too close, phone pushed at the lens. Then a propped phone at table height, medium close-up, with frequent jump cuts.
  - Length: about 56s.
  - **Creator note:** say your real timeframe in place of "two years." Only claim tenure that's true.
- **Alternate hooks (2):**
  1. "Yes, it costs money. Let me tell you what my old way cost me." First frame: man holds his phone in one hand and a crumpled receipt with scribbled math in the other (math unreadable).
  2. "I added up what my mistakes were costing me. Not the app. The mistakes." First frame: him at the table over a notebook full of crossed-out dates.

### Iteration 5: Hi, I'm Your Spreadsheet

- **From:** ad #15 (Jevawell, 4301411716842939). Grade A: running 173 days, still active, 2 versions.
- **Type:** Pattern transfer.
- **What we're borrowing:**
  - The personified lineup of failed fixes in one repeating template: "Hi, I'm X, and all I do is…"
  - The rebuttal recap right before the payoff: "Fibers can't… StoolSoftiners can't… Laxatives can't… but I can."
  - One cause that explains why every fix fails.
  - We drop the animation and the drug naming. One creator plays each tool, holding up a real prop for each, then breaks character.
- **Why it should work for us:**
  - Segment: the beginner. 46% call themselves beginners and 15% brand new. He's a few months in and buried in sources.
  - It's MOFU, so it names the alternatives Michael wants named: spreadsheet, alarm, calorie app, ChatGPT, Reddit and podcasts.
  - It uses the information-overload pain ("The peptides on Reddit are so overwhelming and confusing!").
  - It lands Michael's payoff: "feel accounted for."
  - The belief shift follows the three steps: stack specifics, say "That's a lot," then reveal that the app does it.
- **Setup:**
  - Who: man, 42-50, hoodie.
  - Where: living room couch.
  - How it's shot: phone propped at eye level. For each "character," a jump cut where he holds up the prop (laptop with spreadsheet, phone with alarm, phone with calorie app, laptop with a chat window, phone with a forum thread) in a flat "I'm the villain" voice. Then he drops the act and talks normally.
  - Length: about 58s.
- **Alternate hooks (2):**
  1. "Hi, I'm your phone alarm, and all I do is forget whether you did it." First frame: a ringing alarm screen held right at the lens, his deadpan face behind it.
  2. "Hi, I'm ChatGPT, and I have no idea what you took yesterday." First frame: laptop turned to the camera showing a blank chat window.
