32 winners in 7 lanes, numbered 1 to 32. New this week: a "Breaking into the top 10" lane from daily tracking, which shows where brands moved money in the last few days. ## 1. The big picture

### What runs through everything

**1. GLP-1 side effects are the product now.** (#1, #3, #4, #11, #12, #13, #14, #20)
Zafira says it outright: "On the shot you're eating about half of what you used to. That doesn't run one thing low. It runs the whole list low at once, and a gummy covers one line of it" (#1), and lists "the all day exhaustion, the brain fog waking up at 2am and lying there till 5." Jevawell's headline is "Constipation Due to GLP-1" (#20). Sculptique's mascot ad sends its link to a "glp1-glute-muscle-rebuild" page (#3). PeptidePal's own rising ads sit in the same place: "Of course nothing's moving" (#11), "DAY 5 OF PROTOCOL POOPS GO CRAZY" (#12), "Protein for the days your stomach says no." (#13).
*Why it works on this buyer:* 71% of PeptidePal's tracking devices log a GLP-1-type compound, and the copy lessons already name nausea, constipation and protein on peak days as the pains. The supplement brands sell a pill for each side effect. Only PeptidePal can show when the bad day is coming. Its own ads that say so are the ones outliving its usual 7 to 10 days.

**2. A product that talks.** (#3, #4, #20, #31, plus PeptidePal's cartoons #11 and #12)
A Pixar-style creatine tub at a 50th birthday: "I came with it, so I'm not knocking, I'm going in" (#3). A Zafira bottle in a detective office: "If the bottle doesn't say Zafira with our official logo on it, that is not me" (#4). A prune juice bottle sitting on a blocked colon: "Hi, I'm Prune Juice, and all I do is blow you up" (#20). Everly's skeleton in a branded shirt (#31).
*Why it works:* it stops the scroll in a feed of faces, it keeps the product on screen every second, and it makes private problems (straining, 2am bathroom trips, foam in the toilet) funny instead of gross. Two of this week's five breakouts are animated mascots.

**3. A warning or a confession instead of a pitch.** (#1, #4, #16, #29, and Caitlin's model)
"We need to publicly apologize to every woman on a GLP one who's ever bought a bottle of Zephyra because we lied." (#1). "If you're thinking about orderings of fear of from Amazon, don't, you'll only get scammed." (#4). "This is fake, this is fake, and this is definitely fake." (#16). "Yes, it's a bribe." (#29).
*Why it works:* a warning sounds like a favor, so the viewer's ad defenses drop. It's the same shape as PeptidePal's best own ad ("Do not start peptides if you've just been doing random research on the internet"), which the brand chose as hook style A. Zafira's twist is the sharpest version: the "lie" turns out to be that the discount got bigger, which lets every claim after it run as "yes, it's true."

**4. A time ladder.** (#2, #3, #8, #17, #19, #26, #31)
"Day 30. Your body is recalibrated... Day 60. You're seeing progress... Day 90." (#2). "Week one. You can't see anything yet, and this is where most people quit." (#3). "Week one to month one... by month three... Month six is when it really hit." (#19). "Day 1... Day 3... Day 7. 2am. You're still asleep." (#31). "Week one: 144/98... Week four: 128/82." (#26).
*Why it works:* each step is a small payoff, so long videos hold (76s, 150s, 273s). A slow first step ("this is where most people quit") makes the rest believable. Hone's breakout (#2) shows the ladder is now being used for men too. The brief's buyer wants the same thing: "know exactly where I am this week."

**5. Everything you tried failed, and here's why.** (#1, #3, #18, #19, #20, #24, #25, #26)
Jevawell gives each failed fix a voice: "Hi, I'm Laxatives, and all I do is whip your gut into painful contractions" (#20). "I added everything out that I had spent before on biotin, minoxidil, collagen, supplements, all of it" (#19). "I tried every magnesium supplement out there but they either upset my stomach or didn't work at all" (#24, #25). "You weren't taking pumpkin seed oil. You were taking dead oil in a capsule." (#26). "A regular multivitamin is built for someone eating three meals a day" (#1).
*Why it works:* the buyer isn't blamed; the tool is. PeptidePal's buyer has tried spreadsheets, alarms, Notes, ChatGPT and other trackers, and the brief's common thread is that "the user is the glue, and the glue is where the errors happen." The chosen mechanisms have the same two-part shape: an alarm "only knows *when*, not *whether*", a system "built for one, not two."

**6. One countable, slightly embarrassing moment.** (#8, #11, #12, #16, #17, #22, #30, #31)
"You've been in there 40 minutes." (#11). "I no longer know what getting up five times a night means." (#8). "I had to pee every 90 minutes - even at night" (#22). "Waking up 2-3 times a night to use the bathroom" (#30). "Day 7. 2am." (#31).
*Why it works:* the viewer sees their own life in one detail. The brief has details just as sharp that no winner uses: "Wait, did I take my shot Sunday or Monday?", standing at the fridge counting vials, "I write down what my protein is and then do math."

**7. Proof from the buyer's own numbers.** (#2, #10, #18, #26, #29)
"WE DON'T ASK YOU TO TRUST US. TRUST YOUR SLEEP TRACKER." over red 62 and green 92 sleep-score cards (#10). A testosterone card reading "265 ng/Dl Low" (#2). Hairs "counted them by hand, one by one" (#18). eGFR and blood pressure logs (#26). A refund tied to "ALT, AST, or fibroscan staging" (#29).
*Why it works:* the brief says this buyer respects people who "show receipts: logs, labs, real data." SPNutrition moves the proof off the brand and onto a device the viewer already trusts. PeptidePal *is* that kind of device: its log and history are the receipts.

**8. Take the risk away, then give a reason to act today.** (#1, #3, #4, #6, #7, #8, #18, #19, #21, #23, #24, #25, #27, #28, #29)
"There's a 60 day money back guarantee, with no return required" (#1). "Try a full size product before you buy" (#6, #7, #21). "Best case scenario in 2026, you get your thickness coming back. Worst case scenario, you get your money back." (#19). "DOUBLE DISCOUNT END SOON!" (#27, #28). "NOW OR NEVER" (#23).
*Why it works:* this buyer pays cash and has been burned. The brief's loudest objections are "Bait and switch," "I immediately got charged for $23," and "Says free but you need to pay once all your information is loaded in." No app ad in the set offers anything like a guarantee.

**9. Give something useful first.** (#13, #14, and the quizzes in #6, #18, #27)
PeptidePal's own "FREE PRINTABLE" protein cheat sheets (#13, #14) are rising at 28 days against a 7 to 10 day norm. Kilgour's "free 60-second scalp age assessment" and Spoiled Child's quiz do the same job as a next step.
*Why it works:* a small yes before the ask. The cheat sheets also prove PeptidePal understands the bad days before it asks for a download.

**Emotion across all of them:** recognition first (that's me), then a cause that isn't the viewer's fault, then relief. Fear shows up as one line ("ignoring your kidneys is like waiting until your car is smoking on the highway," #17), and every ad ends on hope or a joke.

Zafira is moving from a before/after static to long video with a story (a fake apology, a scam warning). The wider set adds Loaded Tea ("Why GLP-1 Users Are Obsessed With This Drink"), Vitawild ("If you suffer from dehydration because of your GLP1") and IM8 ("ON A GLP-1? KEEP NUTRITION SIMPLE."). *What it means for PeptidePal:* the GLP-1 side-effect audience is where adjacent brands are spending right now, and they all sell a fix. None of them sells knowing which day it will hit.
- **The script is the asset, not the ad.** Sculptique stopped its "Over 50? You're gonna love this" mascot ad on 2026-10-30 (22 days, 6 versions) and relaunched the same script as a new ad (#3) that broke into its top 10 in 2 days with 5 versions. Its "You have one day" ad from last week's report (Everly's 300-day script with new nouns) is now 31 days old with 13 versions. A proven script gets moved, refreshed and relaunched.
- **The fake confession.** Zafira's "we lied" (#1) is new this week and already #3 in Zafira's top 10. It's a twist on the warning hook: the brand admits something, and the admission is good news.
- **The timeline for men.** Hone's "90 days on TRT" (#2) broke in on day 3. It's the only male-aimed ad in the breakouts, and it uses the Day 30/60/90 ladder the women's supplements have used for months. PeptidePal's real users are 43% men.
- **PeptidePal's own native formats.** The cartoon fiber skit (#11, 29 days), the meme grid (#12, 29 days) and two cheat sheets (#13, #14, 28 days) are all rising. They share one idea: a side effect plus the peak day.
- **Pep AI is moving into GLP-1 progress.** Besides #15, four more Pep AI ads are rising in the wider set: "I thought starting a GLP-1 would be hard until I found this app!", "Best apps to see progress on a GLP.", "On a GLP and still not seeing results, here are some tools to help." and the celebrity hook. **Fading (in the proven ads, but nobody is launching new versions of it):**
- **"Try before you buy" with a quiz.** Spoiled Child's eye-cream ads (#6, #7, #21) are 306 to 366 days old. #21 just came back into Spoiled Child's top 10, but there's no new ad in this style anywhere in the set.
- **Pumpkin seed oil.** Every Everly ad in the report is 232 to 302 days old. Several of them are still in Everly's top 10, so the spend is real, but the newest ideas in the set aren't Everly's.
- **Long-form advertorials.** Everly's 3,000-word letter (#26, 237 days) and Kilgour's 5-minute trial explainer (#18, 186 days) still run. The new long ads this week are videos (#1 at 91s, #3 at 150s), not letters.
- **Static before/after for GLP-1.** Zafira's "ON GLP-1 / WITH ZAFIRA" statics were removed on 2026-11-01. GLP1SOS's "missing manual" statics never lasted past 5 days.
- **PeptidePal's own talking-head and meme winners from last week.** Caitlin's car selfie ("Do not start peptides if you've just been doing random research on the internet") stopped on 2026-10-26 at 59 days. The "$300 vial" meme stopped on 2026-10-26 at 48 days. The first protein timing cheat sheet was removed on 2026-10-25. What's still alive from earlier: "ANOTHER SPREADSHEET" (40 days, 8 versions), the reconstitution cheat card (57 days, #1 in its page's top 10) and the two notes-app posts (55 days). *What it means:* the cheat-sheet format survived the first one being pulled (the new ones are rising), but PeptidePal has no live talking-head winner right now.

### What carries across formats

1. **SPNutrition's "Don't trust us, trust your tracker" static (#10) works as a UGC hook or an LFS opening.** The line moves the proof onto something the viewer owns. In video, the creator holds up their own phone and lets the log speak. In LFS, it's the first line before "see more," followed by a reader's own numbers.
2. **Jevawell's remedy lineup (#20) works as a static grid or an LFS section.** Each failed fix confesses its flaw in the same "Hi, I'm X, and all I do is..." line. On a static, that's a grid of the old tools, each with a one-line confession, and the product in the last box. In LFS, it's the "everything I tried" list that Kilgour (#19) and Everly (#26) already use.
3. **The day counter (#31, #2) works as a carousel, a static or an LFS log.** A big "Day N" over each beat turns a long video into a ladder. As a static it becomes a 3 or 4 step timeline; as LFS it's Everly's "Week one... Week two... Week four" log repeated in the P.S. (#26).
4. **Zafira's "WE LIED..." (#1) works as a static headline.** The red banner and the apologetic face are the whole hook. As a static it's "WE LIED" over the product, with the twist in the first line of the copy.
5. **PeptidePal's meme grid (#12) and cheat sheet (#13) work as video.** The grid is a four-beat skit already: the pain, the caption, the punchline, the fix. The cheat sheet becomes a "save this" talking-head video where the creator walks through the table on a green screen.
