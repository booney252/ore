# PeptidePal Creative Briefs: 3 UGC Ads

Each brief below stands on its own and can go to a different creator. Three changes apply to all three:

- **Length.** The scripts were trimmed to fit the 40-second cap. Each reference read now runs about 140-150 words, so creators need a brisk, venting pace with tight jump cuts.
- **Hook split.** All three use the Oct 24 split: A is Caitlin's protective warning with a physical interrupt, and B is Michael's open loop. No sales commands.
- **Source ads.** No source ad files came with these iterations. The only winner with an ID and a documented reason is Caitlin's ad. Report references (#8, #12, #15 and so on) are cited the way the iterations cited them. Pull their IDs and grade reasons from the report before trafficking.

## Brief 1: Before You Add a Second Peptide

- **From:** Iteration 1, "Before You Add a Second Peptide" (2026-10-21-idea-9ec950-ads-8). UGC video.

- **Objective:**
  - **Funnel and stage:** TOFU, aimed at the problem-aware buyer, the largest group.
  - **Segment:** a woman 45-55 on a weekly shot who has added a second peptide. Payers over-index at 45-54, and 61% of users call themselves new or beginners, so half-life is real news to her.
  - **Mechanism:** this is the lead mechanism, the Second-Compound Cliff.
  - **Belief shift:** from "I can't keep the picture of what's going on in my body straight" to "oh, the app shows me what's still on board."
  - **One-weekly-shot test:** it passes. Even an on-schedule viewer learns that her shot lingers and will stack the moment she adds anything.

- **Placement:**
  - Meta Reels, Stories and Feed.
  - Deliver a 9:16 master (1080x1920) for Reels, Stories and Feed, plus a 4:5 crop (1080x1350) for Feed.
  - Keep all text and the face inside the center 4:5 safe zone.

- **Built from:**
  - **Caitlin's ad, ID 1804184780759088 (PeptidePal, Everyday Wellness page).**
    - What wins: a protective warning given on the viewer's behalf, a physical pattern interrupt (headphones off), one line that names an unspoken feeling, the noise cast as the villain, and the app arriving late (16s).
    - Grade reason: live 56 days and still running. It is PeptidePal's strongest own ad.
  - **Michael's "Half-life" UGC script (written by PeptidePal's owner).**
    - What wins: it teaches half-life in plain words before selling, says "it's not your fault," and brings the product in late.
    - Grade reason: it is the owner's model script. It is not a graded ad.
  - **Report #12, Everly's scam warning.**
    - What wins: the native "warning" disguise that Caitlin's ad also uses.
    - ID and grade: not in this pack.
  - **Report #8, Pep AI.**
    - What wins: a buyer-age face over real app screens.
    - ID and grade: not in this pack.
  - **Anti-model: PeptidePal's dead "Monday dose still doing on Thursday" ad.**
    - It died because it opened on the explanation. Here the warning comes first.

- **Single-minded message:** Every peptide runs on its own half-life, so once you add a second one you can't hold the picture in your head, and PeptidePal shows you what's still on board.

### The ad

**Write it like this:**
- **One moment.** Tuesday, about 9pm, at her kitchen counter under the overhead light. One earbud comes out and her phone lies face-up on the counter.
- **One stake, felt.** She can't tell what's going on in her body once the second peptide is on a different schedule. Then the nausea or constipation hits, and she has no clue why.
- **Two features.**
  - The dose log, checked off when the reminder goes off.
  - The Today screen's estimate of what's still on board.
- **Real nouns.** Use "weekly shot," "second peptide," "half-life," "Today screen" and "reminder." Never use "the new one" or "this thing."
- **Every line earns the next.** The half-life beat follows Michael's order exactly:
  1. "What most people don't realize."
  2. The plain explanation.
  3. One weekly shot is doable.
  4. The second peptide on different days, being late, going up on a dose, and no way to know.
  5. Then the nausea and constipation.
- **No AI rhythm.** Run the half-life explanation as one spoken thought joined with "and" and "which is why." Don't break it into clipped beats.

**Setup:**
- **Who:**
  - A woman 45-55, natural, in a sweatshirt, no glam.
  - She is a real user of a weekly shot plus a second peptide.
  - The nausea and constipation line is in first person, so use it only if it's true for her. If it isn't, she says: "And then people get hit with nausea or constipation and have no clue why."
- **Where:** her real kitchen at about 9pm, overhead light on.
- **How it's shot:**
  - Selfie on her phone, propped or handheld, in one-take style. Jump cuts are fine.
  - Product beat at 29s: green-screen her over a screen recording of the real Today screen from a neutral demo account. Name the items "Weekly shot" and "Peptide 2."
- **Length:** 38-40s and no longer. The reference read is about 140 words, so keep it brisk and cut breaths in the edit.
- **Delivery:**
  - Say the beats in your own words, with your own personality. Never read it word for word.
  - Must-keep:
    - The half-life facts: it stays in your body a while, rises after a dose, then leaves slowly and exponentially, which is why it's called half-life.
    - Nausea and constipation framed only as things you run into, never as something the app fixes.
    - No compound names, and no dose or timing advice.
    - "PeptidePal" plus the real icon in the close.

  - A woman 45-55, real and unpolished, in a sweatshirt with her hair up.
  - She's on a weekly shot and logs food in a calorie app.
  - Cottage cheese can be swapped for whatever high-protein food she really eats.
  - If she's been on the shot a while and wants to say so, she can work in her real tenure ("eight months on my shot and it's nine at night…").
- **Where:** her own kitchen at night, overhead light on.
- **How it's shot:**
  - One take, phone propped on the counter at eye level.
  - One phone-to-lens shot of a generic calorie ring.
  - One cutaway to the PeptidePal screen recording.
- **Length:** 38-40s and no longer. The reference read is about 145 words, so keep it brisk.
- **Delivery:**
  - Say it in your own words, with your own personality, keeping the beats in this order.
  - Must-keep:
    - Protein core: a day or two a week the shot peaks and appetite drops, protein slips, and week after week you're losing weight but keeping the fat and losing muscle.
    - No compound names, ever. That includes the demo account, which shows "Weekly shot."
    - No telling viewers how much or when to dose or eat.
    - No claim the app prevents muscle loss or anything else.
    - "PeptidePal" plus the real icon in the CTA.

**CTA:**
- **Ask:** download PeptidePal from the App Store.
- **Button:** Download.

**Customer language to use.** Use these in your own words. Never show them on screen as PeptidePal reviews.

- "If it isn't my first meal of the day I write down what my protein is and then do math" (Noom review)
- "I do not want to spend hours on an app logging everything, when I am trying to develop a routine that does not center around food." (GlucoPal review)
