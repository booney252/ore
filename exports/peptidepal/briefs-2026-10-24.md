# PeptidePal Creative Briefs: 3 UGC Videos

**Read this first (applies to all three briefs)**

- **Hook split.** Every brief follows the Oct 24 decision. **Arm A** is Caitlin's protective warning with a physical pattern interrupt. **Arm B** is Michael's open loop. Each brief also includes one spare hook for round two. There are **no sales-command hooks**. The Oct 24 decision ("never a sales command") replaces the Oct 22 command test arm, so Brief 2's old "Close the laptop" command hook is now a protective warning.
- **Length.** The format calls for 25-40s. All three run longer: about 51s, 57s and 47s. Michael requires two things that can't fit in 40s at a natural speaking pace: the full five-step half-life explanation and a full protein beat before the product turn. The template allows longer when the source winner needs it, and Caitlin's winning ad runs about 150 spoken words, roughly 50s. The brand's own UGC notes allow up to 60s, and no brief goes past that. **Creators should not speed-read to hit a time.**
- **Scripts are a guide, not a teleprompter.** Each script is written word for word so everyone sees the full ad. Creators deliver the beats **in their own words, with their own personality**. The must-keep items are listed in each brief.
- **Brand rules (the whole list).**
  1. Never say or show an actual compound name. That includes props, fridge labels, spreadsheets and app screens. "Peptide," "GLP-1," "weekly shot," "the pen," "half-life," "nausea" and "the fridge" are all fine.
  2. Link every ad to the **correct** PeptidePal App Store listing, because several apps share the name. The brand supplies the URL. Confirm it before trafficking.
- **Demo account.** The brand provides one neutral demo account for all screen recordings. Items are labelled "Weekly shot," "Morning peptide," "Evening peptide" (Brief 1) and "Weekly shot," "Peptide B" (Brief 2). It should include today's schedule, logged doses, the still-on-board estimate, the protein number and one vial with a run-out date.
- **Source ads.** No source ad files were attached. Caitlin's ad is cited by its real ID. Report-numbered ads (#5, #6, #8, #10, #12, #15, #16, PeptidePal's #7) are cited as the iterations described them. Pull their IDs, advertisers and grade reasons from the ads report before creative review. Where a grade reason wasn't supplied, the brief says so.

## Brief 1: Half, Then Half Again

  - Retimed to a real speaking pace (about 51s, up from the 40s originally claimed for ~170 words).

- **Objective:**
  - **Funnel:** TOFU.
  - **Segment:** men 45-58 who run a weekly shot plus at least one other peptide.
  - **Awareness:** problem aware. They're still on alarms, notes and memory, and they don't know one app can hold all of it.
  - **The belief shift:** from "this is overwhelming, it's literally impossible to know what's going on" to "oh, the app shows me."
  - **Mechanism:** the brand's lead mechanism, the Second-Compound Cliff, explained through half-life.
  - **Goal:** installs.

- **Placement:**
  - Meta Feed, Instagram Reels, Facebook and Instagram Stories.
  - Master is 9:16 (1080×1920). Also deliver a 4:5 (1080×1350) Feed crop.
  - Keep text out of the top 14% and bottom 20% (Reels UI).

### The ad

    1. Dose log with reminders.
    2. The Today screen's estimate of what's still on board.

  - **Every line earns the next.** It moves from the scene, to the cause, to one schedule being fine, to his schedule being impossible, to what that costs him, to "that's a lot," to the relief.

- **Setup:**
  - **Who:** a man, 48-58, with salt-and-pepper stubble and a plain T-shirt. Calm, a little wry. He talks like a peer, not a presenter. He runs a weekly shot plus at least one other peptide in real life.
