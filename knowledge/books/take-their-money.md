# Skill tree: Take Their Money (Kyle Milligan), how to write words that make you rich

Ore's notes on the book's ideas, in our own words, and how they apply to short-form video and organic posts. The book teaches long-form direct response sales letters; the emotions and structures carry over to a 30-second script.

## Root: copy is a language of subtext

- Copy works through what it **communicates**, not the literal words. "Conversational" isn't enough: plain talk doesn't automatically carry intrigue, novelty, safety or urgency. The selling is in the subtext.
- Templates teach you to repeat phrases without knowing why they work. Learn the underlying emotions instead, and the words come naturally.
- **Skill: active reading.** Read winning copy (or watch winning videos) line by line and name what each line is really doing (which emotion, which section job). Binge enough of it and the patterns become obvious, the way you can predict a TV show after bingeing a season.
- **Daily practice:** study one winner a day, write a page (or a script) a day, come up with one idea a day ("what if...").
- Ore applies this when it breaks down a winner: for each line, the subtext it carries, not just what it says.

## Branch 1: The Big 4 emotions (NESB), each with an amplifier

Every line of copy should serve at least one of these. A line can carry several; a piece doesn't need all four, so lean on the two or three that fit the offer.

### 1.1 New → amplified by Only
- **New is the most important emotion.** The viewer's lazy, scanning brain wants to file you as something it's seen before and move on. **Categorization is death**: if they think "I've seen this", you've lost them.
- New's real power is **hope**. The viewer has tried other fixes and they failed. A familiar solution means "this won't work either"; a new one means "maybe this is finally it".
- **New and different beats "better".** "A better version of X" still gets filed as X.
- Signals: breakthrough, discovery, revealed, finally, nobody's talking about this.
- **Only** amplifies it: you can only get this here, from us; nobody else has it. Signals: exclusive, proprietary, only, little-known.
- For scripts: every hook needs something the viewer hasn't seen in their feed. Check competitors' hooks so ours don't sound like theirs.

### 1.2 Easy → amplified by Anybody
- Nobody wants work, and anyone who loves work isn't watching your ad. Make the solution feel push-button: few steps, little time, a system.
- People also feel insecure ("I'm not smart enough, I've failed before"). **Anybody** amplifies Easy: it works even for people who think they're bad at this.
- **"Not" statements** do two jobs:
  1. Intrigue: "It's not X, it's not Y, it's not Z..." makes them need to know what it is.
  2. Remove barriers: "You don't need experience, you don't need money, you don't need to show your face."
- Signals: simple, step-by-step, all you have to do, automatic, anybody, you don't need.

### 1.3 Safe → amplified by Predictable
- Bold promises earn attention but also skepticism. Safe answers the skepticism, and it does a second job: people value what feels safe more highly. Given a sure $80 or an 80% shot at $100, most take the $80. People pay for certainty.
- **Predictable** amplifies Safe: show a pattern ("the last time this happened, here's what followed"), a track record, case studies, testimonials. If you lay out the pattern and let the viewer predict the outcome, they sell themselves.
- Don't claim 100%. A precise, believable number ("93% of the time") feels safer than a perfect one.
- Signals: again, just like before, the last time this happened, history repeating, track record.

### 1.4 Big → amplified by Fast
- The size of the opportunity or change: important, huge, life-changing. Big demands attention and pairs naturally with **FOMO** ("others are already getting it").
- **Fast** amplifies Big: a big result is good; a big result this week is better.
- The other three can feed Big: something new can be disruptive (big); "the last time this happened it changed everything" is Big plus Predictable.

### 1.5 Urgency → amplified by Scarcity (the hot sauce, not a fifth pillar)
- Urgency is a reason to act now (a deadline, "before X happens"). Scarcity limits quantity too ("if there are spots left").
- Use it in small doses through the piece; pour it on at the close.

## Branch 2: The headline (in a video: the hook, frame 0 and on-screen text)

### 2.1 The headline complex
- The eyebrow (a line above), the headline, and the deck (a line below). In a video: the on-screen text, the first spoken line and the caption's first line work as one unit.

### 2.2 The 4 U's
At least two of the four must be there, and Unique is non-negotiable.
- **Urgent:** a reason it matters now (a date, a "before", FOMO).
- **Unique:** something they've never seen (this is New). Highlight one unusual element of the product or story; a familiar thing can be made unique by naming a detail nobody else talks about or giving it a new name. Don't force a template like "the first X to ever...", think about how to make them feel this is new.
- **Useful:** what's in it for the viewer. **Intrigue alone isn't enough**: a curious hook with no payoff for them doesn't hold.
- **Ultra-specific:** odd, precise numbers and details ("$10,251", "12 and a half years younger", "a 3-second move") feel tested and real. Back every specific with something shown later, and never make it up.

## Branch 3: The lead (in a video: the first 3-10 seconds after the hook)

### 3.1 The Soul (one idea)
- Before writing, sum the whole piece up in one sentence: its one big payoff (the "Soul", also called the golden thread or rule of one). Everything that doesn't serve it gets cut. Example: "Trump did a thing, now you get paid."
- The Soul can sharpen as you write, but by the end everything ties to it.

### 3.2 The five jobs of a lead
1. **Capture attention:** a pattern interrupt, something they haven't seen in another ad (a "see this thing I'm holding?" moment).
2. **Expand on the headline:** small payoffs that add detail without giving the secret away, to hold them while you turn to the promise.
3. **Establish credibility:** one line is enough early on (who you are, or an authority who backs the claim).
4. **Make the big promise:** as soon as possible, built from the Big 4 that fit the offer (a system leans Easy and Fast; a trend leans New and Big).
5. **Open a loop:** tease something you'll reveal later so they keep watching ("in a second I'll show you..."). Open the next loop just before closing the last one.
- By the end of the lead, everything that would make them want it has been said; the rest proves it.

## Branch 4: The body (in a video: the middle beats)

### 4.1 The body template: Objection → Claim → Proof → Benefit
Repeat it for each doubt.
- **Objection:** anticipate each "yeah, but...". Generate them from the Big 4: Why haven't I heard of this (New)? Can I even do it (Easy)? How do I know it works (Safe)? How much, how fast (Big)? Plus: why you, why now, what does it cost, what if it doesn't work.
- **Claim:** answer it as a direct statement (often as on-screen text). You don't have to say the objection out loud.
- **Proof:** in threes. Case studies, demonstrations, testimonials, authority quotes, screenshots, numbers. Build up the three so the last is the strongest. **Show, don't tell**, and **reframe** each proof another way (per year instead of per month, compared with something familiar) so it feels real.
- **Benefit:** show the life they get, don't list it ("imagine..."). The deepest benefits are universal private wants: status, revenge on the doubters, ease, pride, being envied, being loved (the seven deadly sins). Stories beat raw data; data switches on the analytical brain and kills momentum.

### 4.2 Common body sections
- **Better than:** this beats the hot, popular thing right now, which sells them away from it.
- **The last time this happened:** a historical pattern, and this time it's bigger (Predictable + Big).
- **Credibility:** why listen to this person (track record, who they know, where they've appeared).
- **Discovery story:** how they found it, usually through an insider or lucky circumstance (Only).
- **Key insight:** the one fact that has to be true for this to work, plus signs it's already happening.
- **Layman's explanation:** how it works, simple enough for two tipsy friends at a bar to explain to each other.
- **Technical explanation:** the same thing in a lab coat. They don't need to follow it; they need to see you do.

## Branch 5: The offer (in a video: the CTA and what happens next)

- Recap the hype, then introduce what they get. **Tease what's inside, never the details**: information isn't exciting, and if they think they've figured out the secret they'll do it themselves or find it cheaper.
- Give ordinary things new names (a dossier, not a report). Even standard features should feel new.
- **Unique selling proposition:** something truly unique, something common nobody else talks about (every brand's connectors are gold-plated, but only one said so), or an old thing with a new name.
- **What to expect:** make the next step concrete and easy (what they'll get, when, how little it takes).
- **Bonuses:** related to the main promise, never random add-ons that cheapen it.
- **Testimonials:** people who look and live like the viewer, with specifics (the more detail, the more of the Big 4 they carry).
- **Value recap and price anchor:** stack the value, then the price. Keep the anchor believable (under about 20x the price). Compare against something much pricier.
- **Guarantee, scarcity, deadline.**
- **Crossroads close:** give two paths (join and get the new life, or go back to how things were) and let them choose. People want the choice to be theirs; don't call them stupid for saying no.
- A P.S. that re-sells the whole thing in brief.

## How Ore uses this
- **Breakdowns:** name the Big 4 each winning line carries, and the subtext under the words.
- **Hooks:** must be new (no categories they've seen), useful (what's in it for them) and ideally specific; urgency is optional.
- **Scripts:** have one Soul. Run hook → payoff → credibility → big promise → open loop in the first seconds. Run objection → claim → proof (3, shown) → benefit in the middle. End with a crossroads-style CTA.
- **Proof:** where a script makes a claim, give it proof to show and a reframe, and never invent the proof.
