# Skill tree: Michael Masterson, "Fear and Greed: Two Overrated Emotions" (plus a note on outlining and editing)

Ore's notes on the essays' ideas, in our own words, and how they apply to our copy.

## Branch 1: Choosing the emotion

### 1.1 Fear and greed are overrated
- An old direct-marketing rule says to sell with fear and greed. It's wrong as a rule: there are many stronger and more precise motivators.
- **Fear** is primal and strong, but its natural responses are to fight, run or freeze, and we want none of those. Too much fear lowers response.
  - Lead with fear only when the product is protection (insurance, alarms, monitors, backups).
  - Everywhere else, use a little fear (a real risk, a real stake) and follow it **quickly with hope**.
- **Hope is a far stronger selling emotion than fear.** (This matches *Take Their Money*: new = hope.)
- **Greed** attracts the wrong buyers: gullible, refund-heavy and disloyal. Greed-led campaigns can win big once and still sink the business through refunds, churn and buyers who never buy again. Good customers want success and more money, but they don't see themselves as greedy.

### 1.2 What to appeal to instead (Cialdini's persuasion principles)
- **Reciprocity:** people want to give back when you give first (real value up front: a useful tip, a free step, a demo).
- **Commitment and consistency:** once someone takes a small step, they want to stay consistent with it (a small yes first: comment, follow, save, "if that's you, keep watching").
- **Social proof:** people look at what others like them are doing (real members, real numbers, crowds already in).
- **Liking:** people say yes to people they know and like (a creator who feels like a friend, shares the viewer's story, admits flaws).

### 1.3 How this fits Ore's aggressive house style
- Aggressive means bold, specific and confident, not fear-mongering or greed-baiting.
- Money copy: frame money as **success, freedom, security, providing, proving the doubters wrong**, not as greed or easy riches. AICL's research agrees: viewers mock flexing and luxury spending; "a stable 10k a month" lands, "get rich" reads as a scam.
- Fear copy: name the real stake in one beat, then turn to hope and the way out. Never end on fear.
- When choosing a script's main emotion, ask what the best long-term buyer wants to feel about themselves after buying.

## Branch 2: Outline and edit (from the second essay)
- A master can scale through others: the master supplies the outline and does the final edit for **pace and tension**; others draft. Quality comes from the outline and the edit, not the first draft.
- For Ore: a strong outline (the Soul, the beats, the bags) is what makes a script good. When editing a draft, cut for pace and tension first.
- A writer's two vices are **ego** (thinking only you can write it, refusing feedback) and **laziness** (skipping the research and the edit). Feedback loops (copy lessons) exist to beat the first; the research brief exists to beat the second.

## How Ore uses this
- Pick a main emotion other than greed. Use fear only as a short stake for non-protection products, then hope.
- Build in at least one Cialdini lever per script: give first (reciprocity), a small yes (commitment), others like them (social proof), a likable, relatable creator (liking).
- Money promises speak to success, freedom and security, not greed.
