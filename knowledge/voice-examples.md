# Voice examples

Scripts the owner wrote by hand. Match how they sound (the rhythm, the plain words, how sentences run together), not their topic or brand. When a line you write sounds more polished than these, rewrite it to sound like these.

## AI Creator Lab explainer (organic, talking to camera, said by Finn, a founder)

If you're wondering what AI Creator Lab actually is, this is for you. I'm Finn by the way, one of the founders.

We are a network of creators running faceless AI influencer pages, partnering up with 8 and 9 figure Amazon brands.

You build a page around an AI avatar, post AI generated short form videos, and in those videos you promote these Amazon products from brands people already trust.

You never show your face, never get on camera, and never even touch a product.

You get paid two ways: commission on every sale which can be upwards of 100% commission, plus monthly guaranteed retainers from the brands so you get paid just to post.

And honestly these brands have more budget than there are creators to spend it on. And they want to spend it on you.

Inside, we connect you with these brands and the best-paying products, and you get coached by high level creators already doing this full time.

[pop up a real creator's win on screen]: (creator name) started X months ago, and hit a $X month… (or whatever win you want to show)

There's a ton more wins on this page, so feel free to check them out.

But if you don't want to waste any more time, DM us the word "INFO" to learn more about how you can get started.

**Why it sounds human:**
- **Asides and connectors:** "by the way", "And honestly", "so", "But if…". Sentences start with "And".
- **Long sentences that run on and carry several facts,** next to short ones. The lengths are uneven.
- **Plain, specific words:** "8 and 9 figure Amazon brands", "upwards of 100% commission", "get paid just to post".
- **It talks straight to "you" and walks through how it works in order.** No slogans, no fragments, no self-answered questions.
- **Its one list of three** ("never show your face, never get on camera, and never even touch a product") is a natural spoken sentence with "and", not three clipped fragments. That's the difference.

## PeptidePal "Half-life" (UGC, talking to camera, written by Michael, PeptidePal's owner)

If you're on more than one peptide, let me tell you something that's literally life changing, because holy ****

Okay, so let's say you have one peptide every morning. A second peptide every other night. Maybe a third peptide, split every four days.

Most people don't understand why it is so important to track these things. It's not just to stay tidy and on top of your life, but it's how your body literally processes the compounds and breaks them down. Where it gets crazy is this concept called half-life, so hear me out on why you need to know this.

Maybe you already remember this from your high school chemistry class, but what this means is that each peptide stays in your body for a while. It doesn't just disappear after you take it. The dose takes a while to reach its peak in your blood stream, but then it also leaves your body slowly too.

It's a whole mathematical thing, it literally exponentially decreases over time, which is where the term half-life comes from. And guess what, each substance has its own timing.

So now let's say you're taking your peptides. You're taking them on different days, each one has a different half-life. Maybe some days you're late, so your whole half-life curve shifts. Or you're ramping up on dosage, so the math gets even harder.

… It's literally impossible to know where your levels are at. That's where it can get dangerous and crazy.

Like many people, you might start dealing with side effects with minimal understanding of what's going on, and how to solve them.

Constipation, nausea, anhedonia, or you're losing weight but it's only muscle that you're losing. You're entirely in the dark.

[pause] What if I told you it's not your fault, or something wrong with your body. What if all you needed to do was track the half-life curves. To know where your peaks and troughs are.

If you know when the dosage is peaking, you can prepare: you can drink lots of water beforehand, you can take dietary supplements, you can make sure that you've already hit your protein goals before your appetite goes to zero.

I've been taking peps for years/months/etc. But I only just discovered half-life not too long ago. When I brought it up to my friends, I was surprised that none of them really knew or cared about it either. So obviously the group chat went down a whole rabbit hole.

We started making spreadsheets together, asking ChatGPT questions, sharing knowledge, yadda yadda, and it helped.

But then I'm tracking my doses AND checking spreadsheet formulas. Like, I have somehow given myself homework.

That's when I discovered this app. It's called PeptidePal… It pretty much just does everything for me. I put my doses in, and it does the half-life math and shows me where my medication levels are at. It alerts me when things are peaking. It tells me how to prepare for the worst days.

Now I can actually follow what's going on. Now, I'm no longer spending my evening checking whether I broke a formula on the group spreadsheet.

It's been an absolute game changer and I've never been happier. I put all my friends on, and they've been loving it too! If you're taking peps, or you don't know your own half-life levels, download PeptidePal!

**Why it sounds human:**
- **It talks like he's explaining it to a friend:** "Okay, so let's say…", "so hear me out", "Maybe you already remember this from your high school chemistry class", "And guess what".
- **Casual filler real people use:** "yadda yadda", "Like, I have somehow given myself homework", "pretty much just does everything for me".
- **It teaches before it sells.** Half-life gets explained in plain words, step by step. Then the pain, then "it's not your fault", then the fix.
- **A real story with a turn:** the group chat goes down a rabbit hole, spreadsheets help, then the spreadsheet becomes homework, then the app.
- **The product shows up late,** after the viewer understands the problem.
