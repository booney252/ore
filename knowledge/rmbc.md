# RMBC notes

My notes from the RMBC II course, in my own words. Private: never shipped to other users.

## How to think about copy

- **Context is king.** Copy never exists in a vacuum. Ads look, feel and read differently on each platform; match what the viewer was doing (a video on YouTube, a native-looking post on Reels or TikTok) so the ad blends in.
- We're copy thinkers more than copywriters: AI does most of the heavy writing, we're the commander.
- **Direct response** means the viewer acts right away: click, buy, sign up, comment, share. Target people who already have the behavior or the problem. A coffee brand with "energy without the crash" sells to coffee drinkers, not to people it hopes to convert to coffee.
- Two journeys to plan for:
  1. Prospect to purchaser (point A, not a customer, to point B, paying customer).
  2. Customer to repeat champion (point B to point C, a loyal customer who brings others).
- There's never perfect information. It's all testing.
- The simplest way to write effective copy is to model what's already working for successful brands (swiping), then make it ours.

## Funnels

- Awareness (the ad, organic discovery, a recommendation) > Interest (click) > Desire (the page) > Action (purchase).
- Core flows: Ad > VSL > Checkout; Ad > Advertorial > VSL > Checkout; Ad > Advertorial > VSL > PDP > Checkout; Ad > PDP > Checkout; Ad > Advertorial > PDP > Checkout.
- VSL shape: hook in 5 seconds with curiosity; build rapport and credibility; a hero's-journey story; the unique mechanism of the problem (UMP) and of the solution (UMS); the product as the best way to get the UMS; build perceived value; reveal the price as a great deal; tell them to buy.

## Research

- Stack the research: market awareness + competitor research + psychographics, then one brief that feeds everything.
- Read the full research at least once. Summaries miss the small insights that end up mattering.

### Market awareness (5 stages)

- Know where the majority sits; there's always variance. The message changes at every stage.
- **Unaware** (often ~40%): don't know they have the problem. Not a direct response target; they need nurturing, and convincing someone they have a problem is hard.
- **Problem aware**: know the problem, don't know the solutions.
- **Solution aware**: know solutions exist, not our product.
- **Product aware**: know which kind of product solves it and are weighing it (still not necessarily ours). The line between solution and product aware is subtle.
- **Most aware** (under 1%): know us, about to buy.
- Usually market to problem and solution aware. When they're solution or product aware, teach why ours is the best way to solve it.
- **Business opportunities sell best to problem aware (or unaware) people.** An Amazon FBA course targets people who want out of their 9-5 and want to make money, not people who already know what FBA is.

### Competitor research

- Most important: their core messaging (if they're winning, parts of it work for us; look for what most of them share), customer sentiment, and what customers like and dislike. Direct response wins on core messaging.

### Psychographics

- Demographic = the facts (age, gender, income, married, own or rent). Psychographic = the psychology (what they believe, want, fear).
- **Attitudes** (economic, political, social, religious) change the copy completely. Is money the root of evil, earned through hard work, or luck?
- **Hopes and dreams**: the deeper outcome, not the surface one ("no wrinkles" is really "confident and sexy in my own skin").
- **Victories and failures**: what they've tried, what worked, what flopped, why.
- **Outside forces**: who or what they blame (Big Pharma, the food system, the government). In copy: "You may feel like the deck is stacked against you..."
- **Prejudices**: who they distrust (older women don't trust a 25-year-old beauty rep; young people resent boomers).
- **Core beliefs** about life, love and family, in a sentence or three.
- **Existing solutions**: the whole market, not just direct competitors (weight loss: supplements, gyms, GLP-1s, coaching, diets). What they like and hate; horror stories are ad ideas; do they think it works? Even if it does, position against cost, hassle or side effects.
- **Curiosity**: unique or old fixes that worked and got forgotten or suppressed.
- **Corruption ("paradise lost")**: the problem used to not exist or not be this bad, and an outside force made it worse. The message: it's not your fault. (Solis: mornings used to be yours, then addictive apps took them.)

## Mechanism

- A unique mechanism is a two-part logical connection that explains why the prospect has struggled and how they can finally win.
- By the time they find us they've tried other fixes that didn't work, worked partly, or stopped working. Our job is to show the real reason: their picture of the problem was missing one crucial piece.
- **UMP** (mechanism of the problem): why they've failed before.
- **UMS** (mechanism of the solution): why our product works where the others didn't.
