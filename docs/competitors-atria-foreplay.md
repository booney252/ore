# Atria and Foreplay: what they do and don't do

Researched Oct 29, 2026 from their sites, help centers, pricing pages and reviews. Some reviews were written by their competitors; those are marked.

## The short version

| | Atria | Foreplay | Ore today |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | All-in-one: ad library, your own ad analytics, AI assistant (Raya) that writes briefs and makes image ads, Meta ad launcher | Research and organizing: swipe file, ad library, competitor tracking, your own ad analytics, brief builder | Research engine plus writer: competitor tracking, breakdowns, customer research, briefs and scripts that learn |
| **Price** | Free plan; $129 / $343 / $959 a month (annual) | $59 / $175 / $459 a month | Not released |
| **Platforms** | Meta, TikTok | Saves Meta, TikTok, LinkedIn; tracking Meta only | Meta ads; organic IG, TikTok, Shorts |
| **Traction** | Claims 20,000+ teams; about $2.1M seed (Kleiner Perkins, Accel per Caplight); 29 people | Claims 10,000+ teams; no funding found; one estimate is about $1.2M a year | Two brands, dogfooding |
| **Reviews** | G2 5.0 (22); Trustpilot 3.0 (8: billing, cancel button, support, "plastic" AI images) | G2 4.8 (about 126); "expensive" is the top complaint | n/a |

## Spotting winning competitor ads (the top-10 idea)

- **Atria already ranks each competitor ad by impressions** within its advertiser ("Top 2%", "#4 of 212"), with a chart of the rank over time, its best rank, and an "Impression growth" filter. This is very close to our top-10 tracking. What I couldn't find: an **alert** when a new ad breaks into the top, or a "breakout" flag. Real reach and spend numbers only exist for ads shown in the EU or UK.
- **Foreplay only uses days running** ("sort by longest running") and active status. Its Spyder feature tracks competitors' Meta ads 24/7, keeps them forever, shows a test timeline and exports hooks, and sends Slack or email updates (how often isn't stated). It has no impression ranking and no breakout signal.

**What this means:** the top-10 idea isn't unique. Atria has the data underneath it. The gap is turning it into a **signal**: "this new ad broke in, held for days, is being copied into new versions, and other brands are on the same angle," pushed to you as an alert with the reason.

## Breaking down why an ad works

- **Atria:** transcribes and searches spoken words and on-screen text, plus vague "AI analysis of ad scripts." Its detailed AI tagging (persona, desire, hook, offer and so on) only works on **your own** ads.
- **Foreplay:** free transcripts, plus an automatic "emotional analysis, targeting, persona" note on every saved ad. Hooks are just the start of the transcript.
- **Neither** takes a competitor's winner apart element by element (frame 0, first 3 seconds, structure, proof, offer, pacing) and says which elements carry it and why it's still running. Ore's report does.

## Customer research

- **Atria:** mines reviews (Trustpilot) and the help docs say Raya "analyzes customer reviews." Reviewers called this "unique." No library of exact quotes with links, and no psychographic or market awareness research.
- **Foreplay:** none.
- **Ore:** exact quotes with sources, comments, a full research brief, segments, awareness. This is the biggest gap in both.

## Briefs and scripts

- **Atria:** Raya writes briefs, concepts and copy using a "Brand World": guidelines, tone, personas, product and competitor profiles, drafted from your URL. A reviewer called the output "a starting point" for copywriters. It also makes image ads (Trustpilot: "looking plastic and inhuman").
- **Foreplay:** a brief and storyboard builder with AI script writing from a brand profile plus ads you pick by hand. One G2 reviewer: "haven't had much luck with the briefing yet."
- **Neither** has a documented way of **learning from feedback** on its briefs. Atria's chat memory only lasts for one chat. Neither writes for a specific creator. Ore's copy lessons (Michael's notes becoming rules) and creator profiles (Ashley) are both things they don't have.

## Your own ad results

- **Both connect to your Meta account** (Atria also TikTok, Shopify, Triple Whale, Northbeam). Atria's Radar grades your last 30 days of ads into winners and iteration candidates and writes a brief to scale them. Foreplay's Lens scores your ads against your own and against 20,000+ advertisers.
- **Ore doesn't do this yet.** It's the one area where both are clearly ahead.

## Other things they have that Ore doesn't

- Chrome extension to save any ad in one click
- Boards, sharing with clients, white-label reports (Foreplay)
- Launching ads to Meta and making image ads (Atria)
- Slack and Notion connections, an API and MCP (both)
- TikTok ad tracking (Atria)

## Things neither does

- Alerts on new competitor ads breaking into the top
- Organic outliers (Reels, TikTok, Shorts versus the account's usual views)
- Element-by-element breakdowns saying why a competitor's winner wins
- Customer quotes with sources, and a full research brief
- Learning from feedback on its scripts and briefs, per brand
- Scripts written for a specific creator

## Takeaways for Ore

1. **Don't compete on the library, ad saving or your-own-ad analytics.** They're ahead and cheap.
2. **The top-10 data alone isn't a moat.** Atria already ranks by impressions. The alert plus the "why" plus the proof is what's missing.
3. **Ore's real edge is depth:** why it wins, what customers actually say, and a writer that remembers each client's notes. That matches the "Ore finds and proves, the strategist writes" direction.
4. **Their weak spots are trust and billing:** cancel buttons that don't work, double charges, slow support. Being easy to cancel and responsive is cheap to win on.
